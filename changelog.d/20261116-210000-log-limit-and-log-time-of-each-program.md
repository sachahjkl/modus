**A log limit and a log time that the other programs do not set**: a program that read its own log records could still learn how much the other programs wrote, from a `Log` that the kernel refused while its own count was low, or from the time of a `Log`. The log records of each second are now an amount that a budget holds, as its pages: a child budget takes them from its starter with its part of the count of this second, and gives both back at its end; a budget counts only its own records, and a `Log` spends each record that its text takes. `Budget_Read` gives the records that a budget still holds after its children. The machine has 959 records in each second and the kernel 64 of its own, half of the whole log, so the serial port loses no line while the device keeps their rate: the root service keeps 120, gives a row of its table to 9 programs and 7 to each other program, and a starter gives a child that writes 4. `system.log` never waits: a refused line is dropped and counted, and the next accepted line comes after one record "<count> lines of this program were refused by its log limit". `Log` no longer writes the serial port: the tick of each processor writes the lines of the new records in their order, only while the device takes bytes and for 100 µs at most, and the kernel writes the lines that are left before the machine stops, before the report of a fault, and in a wait for a lock longer than 1 s. Each scenario of the tool fails on a line lost on the serial port.
