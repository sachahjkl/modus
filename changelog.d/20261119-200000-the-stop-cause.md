**The kernel records why a process stopped**: each stopped process has a stop cause that no other process can forge: itself, a holder of its process handle, the end of its last thread, the close of its last handle, a fault, a page that is not committed, a return that the processor refused, or a fault that no thread can answer. `Process_State` gives it as its second result, `monitor.processes()` shows it in its column `cause`, and the lines name it: "root: settings stopped: itself, code 0", "root: net stopped: a holder", "error: the process calendar stopped: a fault, page fault". The codes 0x100 to 0x500 of the kernel are gone: a stop of the kernel has the code 0. The demonstrations and the tests stop with 0 or 1 and give their results through memory that they share with their parent; the start policies still stop with their own codes until the stop outcome replaces the code, and the kernel does not check the code yet.
