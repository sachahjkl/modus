**The access records of `server` are a record output, and a slow reader slows or loses with a count**: `server` writes the record of each request into its record output `requests`, which a reader wires, in place of a line of the log with a limit of 100 in each second. In the console, after `connect("server")`, `server.requests |> where(status = 404) |> first(5)` gives the requests of the status 404 from the next one on, and "joined after N records" counts those before. `server` never waits for a reader of the console: a full ring refuses the next records with `Full`, and `stream_counts(server.requests)` now says `held` beside `written`, `discarded` and `refused`: the writes that a full ring held until it had room, which a write that then gave `Full` counts in both. It also says `rings_past_gap`: the readers whose gap reached its largest count, whose ring the writer closed. On a ring with a condition, a gap counts 2^31 − 1 records at most, so a reader always tells a record ahead from one behind. A reader wired with the pace `Always_Holds_Writer` holds the server and loses nothing; one wired with the pace `Never_Holds_Writer` reads a gap record with the exact count of the records that it lost, and the other readers are not touched. The log has no access record of `server` any more.
