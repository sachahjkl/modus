**The serial port keeps up with an idle machine**: since `Log` stopped writing the serial port, only the ticks wrote it, 100 µs at each, about 4.5 KB in each second for a processor under KVM, where a port write costs 22 µs; the boot writes about 120 KB of lines, so the serial port lagged the system by seconds, and a test that acted on the moment of a line acted too late. A processor with nothing to run now writes the serial port at the rate of the port, with a window for interrupts after each byte, and leaves at once when a thread becomes ready or an interrupt comes. On a machine with an idle processor, a line reaches the serial port about when its program goes on. A machine whose processors are all busy still drains at the ticks only. The measure line of the drain now says the bytes of the idle loop and of the ticks.
