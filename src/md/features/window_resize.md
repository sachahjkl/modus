# Windows that the principal resizes

The principal drags an edge or a corner of a window, and the process draws its next frame at the new size. The guide is [`docs/DESIGN.md`](../../../docs/DESIGN.md), "Windows"; the protocol is in `src/library/modus/display`.

## Constraints

- **The compositor never waits for a process.** A process that draws slowly, or never, must not slow a frame, the input or another window.
- **A process never draws into a surface that the compositor resized under it.** The memory of a surface is the memory of the process; its size changes only when the process says so.
- **The budget of the process pays for its surface** ([`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "14. Memory is supplied by the caller": whoever creates an object pays for it). A budget that cannot pay is the status `No_Budget`, which the process reads.
- **A window that the process did not draw for a size never shows at that size.** The screen shows whole frames ([../infrastructure/whole_frame.md](../infrastructure/whole_frame.md)).
- **The process decides.** A process whose layout has fixed sizes stays fixed, and one whose texts need a width states that width as its minimum: no text is ever cut.

## The chosen form

### The declaration: resizable or fixed

A window is fixed unless its process declares it resizable. After `display.open`, the process calls `display.resizable_set(&window, limits)`: the least and the largest width and height of its surface. The command `Resizable` carries them. The compositor checks them at its border (`display.size_limits_of_words`):

- each least size is at least `RESIZABLE_LEAST_WIDTH` × `RESIZABLE_LEAST_HEIGHT` (the title must hold the close button and a part of the title);
- each largest size is at most `SURFACE_LIMIT`;
- each least size is at most its largest size;
- the size of the surface that the process has is inside the limits: the surface of a `Surface_Resize` that waits for its `Present`, or else the surface on the screen.

The compositor refuses limits that break a rule, with one line of the log; the window stays as it was. A second `Resizable` replaces the limits. An answer to a request that comes after narrowed limits is checked against the new limits, on both sides, because the two records cross the ring in order.

### The drag

- **The edges.** The pointer is at an edge when it is on the border of the window, or at most `RESIZE_REACH` pixels outside its frame. A point near two edges is at a corner. The title bar is not an edge, but its left and right columns are the left and right edges, as the border goes up through it; the band above it is the top edge.
- **Which window takes the press** (`bar.resize_target`). A point inside the frame of a window belongs to the first such window from the front: its border, when it is resizable, else no edge. The band outside a frame takes a point only where no window lies: the band of a window in front never takes a press from a window behind it.
- **One request at a time for each window.** From the release until the process answers, the edges of the window take no new drag, and give no resize shape. The process answers each `Resized` once, so the wait ends at its next picture at the latest (see "The new surface"). A process that never reads its events never answers, and then only its own window loses its edges. A bound of time was refused: an answer after the bound would come for a request that the compositor no longer has, and the process would draw into a surface that the screen never shows.
- **The pointer shape.** Over an edge of a resizable window, the shape is `resize_left_right`, `resize_up_down`, `resize_top_left_bottom_right` or `resize_top_right_bottom_left`. During the drag, the shape stays the one of the edge.
- **The size.** The pointer moves the edges that it holds, to the pixel. The new size is cut to the limits of the window. An edge never goes past the screen: not left of 0, not right of the screen, not over the bar, not below the screen. A window that is already partly off the screen is never made larger in that direction, and never made smaller by the screen alone. The pointer is on the screen at each moment, so a drag outside the screen is a drag to its edge.
- **The outline.** During the drag, the compositor draws the outline of the new frame, 2 pixels wide, in the color of the title of the window in front, over the windows and under the pointer. The window keeps its picture and its frame. No surface is made during the drag: one size goes to the process, at the release.
- **The release.** The compositor sends the event `Resized` with the new surface size, when it differs from the size now. It keeps the request: the asked size, and which edges the drag held, so that the new surface keeps the opposite edges in place.
- **The maximize.** The button of the title bar, the entry "Maximize Window" of the menu of the desktop and Control+Shift+Up toggle one state (`desktop_toggle_maximize`). The window goes to the top left corner of the work area, the screen less the bar, and asks for the size that fills it, within its limits: the one `Resized`, with the right and the bottom edges held. The second toggle gives back the place and the size of before, with a `Resized` too. A window that is not resizable only moves. While a request of the window waits for its answer, the toggle changes nothing: the answer would give the window a size under the new one. A snap to a half of the screen asks for its size the same way.
- **The corners.** Within `bar.CORNER_REACH` (14 logical pixels) of a corner, an edge takes the edge beside it too, and the pointer has the diagonal shape. At the bottom right corner the square of that size inside the frame is the corner too: it holds the grip that the compositor draws there (`bar.GRIP_SIZE`). No other corner has such a square: the buttons of the title bar are at the top, and the surface of the process keeps its pixels at the bottom left.
- **A restore while a request waits.** A restore that comes while the window waits for the answer to its maximize puts the window back at its place at once, and asks for its size of before when that request ends: at its new surface, its decline or its refusal (`desktop_restore_size_after_answer`). The compositor writes "window ... maximized" and "window ... restored" at each toggle.
- **The restore button.** While a window is maximized, its maximize button shows two windows, one behind the other: the restore. The palette names its entry of the window in front "restore window" then, and "maximize window" else; the menu of the desktop says "Restore Window" or "Maximize Window"; the plates of the bar say "maximized".
- **The double press and the drag of a maximized window.** Two presses on a title bar, on one window, at most 400 ms apart and at most 4 logical pixels apart on each axis, toggle the maximize the same way (`bar.double_press_take`); the system has no setting of that time, so 400 ms is the usual time of desktops. A third press starts a new pair, and a press anywhere else forgets the first. A drag of the title of a maximized window gives it back its size of before once the pointer leaves the press by more than the same distance: the window asks for that size, and the pointer keeps the same part of its title (`bar.restored_hold_x`), so the drag goes on with the smaller window under the pointer.
- **The keys.** Control+Shift+F8, or the entry "resize window" of the palette, is a drag of the corner at the bottom right by the arrow keys: each key is one step of 16 logical pixels, or 1 with Shift, through the same rule of the size (`bar.keyboard_resize_size` calls `bar.resize_drag`). Enter is the release: the one `Resized`, with the right and the bottom edges held. Escape asks nothing. The window enters the mode only when its edges would take a drag ([accessibility.md](accessibility.md), "3. Keyboard access to everything").

### The new surface

- The process reads `Resized` from `display.next_event`. The border (`display.event_of_record`) refuses a size of 0 or past `SURFACE_LIMIT`.
- `next_event` keeps the request in the window. The process calls `display.surface_resize(&window, width, height)` with the size of the event; another size, or no request, gives `Bad_Operation`. It makes a new memory object of two pictures of the new size, paid by its own budget; gives the compositor a handle with Read; sends `Surface_Resize`; then unmaps and closes the old surface. `window.canvas` is a picture of the new surface, which the compositor does not read: the process draws into it at once.
- A budget that cannot pay gives the status `No_Budget` and its error record. The window keeps its old surface and its size, the display package writes one line, and it sends `Resize_Declined`, the other answer. The compositor then takes a drag of the window again.
- The interface package does this in `window_events`, and draws the next frame whole. A process that draws with the interface package reads its size from `window.width` and `window.height`, not from constants.
- **The units.** The size of `Resized`, of `surface_resize` and of the limits is logical pixels, at the scale in force; the surface and the size of `Surface_Resize` are pixels of the screen at the scale of the window ([desktop_to_browser.md](desktop_to_browser.md), "The reference screen"). The compositor compares the size of `Surface_Resize` with the asked size and the limits at its scale, in pixels of the screen.
- **A new scale is no resize.** It has a state machine of its own, below: the window keeps its logical size and its place, and changes the pixels of its surface.

### The rescale: one state machine on each side

The window and the compositor each follow one state machine, and three facts must never disagree: the surface that the window draws, the surface that the compositor shows or holds, and the scale that each side uses. So the window rescales only to the scale that an event of the compositor names, and each request of the window gets an answer.

**The facts that cross the ring.**

- `Settings_Changed` carries the scale of the compositor (the word of its count of eighths). The window rescales to that scale, never to a read of the page: the page can change again before the compositor reads it. The compositor sends it at each change of its scale, and to a window whose `Surface_Create` names another scale than its own.
- Each surface has a **surface serial**: 1 for the surface of `Surface_Create`, one more for each surface after it. `Surface_Resize`, `Surface_Rescale` and `Surface_Release` name a serial, and each `Present` names the serial of the surface that it shows. The compositor takes a `Present` of the serial that it shows, or of the serial that it holds and has not shown yet, which it then shows; it refuses and counts each other one. So a `Present` never names pictures of a surface that the compositor does not have.
- `Surface_Answer` is the one event of the answers: `Released` (the compositor maps nothing of that serial any more), `Rescale_Taken`, `Rescale_Refused`, `Resize_Taken` and `Resize_Refused`. Each request of a surface gets one answer, with its serial.
- An answer never goes lost. When the ring of the window is full, the compositor keeps it in one slot of the window (`bar.Answer_Slot`) and sends it first when the ring has room, before the events that wait. One slot is enough, since a window has one request at a time; a second answer does not replace the first. An answer can pass older events: its serial, not its place, says which request it ends. Without the slot, a lost `Released` would leave the window in Releasing for good, drawing nothing.

**The window** (`display.Rescale_Machine`, a pure procedure `rescale_step` with host tests):

| Phase | The window | Input | Next phase, and what the window does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady | draws its surface of scale s and serial n | a scale w ≠ s | Releasing: sends `Surface_Release(n)`, draws nothing |
| Steady | | a scale w = s | Steady |
| Releasing | draws nothing | a scale w | Releasing: w is the scale wanted |
| Releasing | | `Released(n)` | Offered: unmaps and closes its surface, makes the surface of the scale wanted and serial n + 1 from its budget, sends `Surface_Rescale`, presents it filled with the background, draws into it |
| Releasing | | `Released(n)`, and the budget cannot pay the surface | Released at n + 1, with one fact: sends `Surface_Declined(n + 1, w)`, holds no picture, draws nothing; Offered at the next scale that differs |
| Offered | draws the offered surface, presents it | a scale w | Offered: w is the scale wanted |
| Offered | | `Rescale_Taken(n + 1)` | Steady; and Releasing at once when the scale wanted differs |
| Offered | | `Rescale_Refused(n + 1)` | Released, with one fact; Offered again at once when the scale wanted differs from the refused one |
| Released | draws nothing; the compositor draws the frame with the background | a scale w | Offered, a surface of scale w |
| Steady | | `surface_resize` to a size that the principal asked | Resizing: makes the surface of the new size and serial n + 1, sends `Surface_Resize`, keeps the surface of serial n mapped, draws into the new one |
| Resizing | draws the new surface, presents it | a scale w | Resizing: w is the scale wanted |
| Resizing | | `Resize_Taken(n + 1)` | Steady at n + 1: lets the surface of serial n go; Releasing at once when the scale wanted differs |
| Resizing | | `Resize_Refused(n + 1)` | Steady at n, with one fact: lets the new surface go and draws the surface of serial n again, at its size, which the compositor still shows; Releasing at once when the scale wanted differs |

An answer of another serial is the answer of an older request: it changes nothing. `surface_resize` is taken in Steady only; in the other phases the window declines the request. The window keeps the surface before a resize until the answer, so a refusal has a surface to go back to; this costs no page more than before, since the compositor mapped both until the `Present` anyway.

**The compositor**, for each window: **Shown** (a surface of serial n), **Pending** (a second surface, of a resize or a rescale, that the next `Present` of its serial shows), **Released** (no surface; the frame and the background of the window). `Surface_Release(n)` unmaps each surface of the window, shown or pending, ends a request of the principal, records n as the serial released, and is always answered with `Released(n)`. It names the newest serial of the window, which can be one past the surface that the compositor shows when it refused a `Surface_Resize`: the release then brings the two sides back to one serial. `Surface_Resize` names the next serial too, and its pixels are the asked size at the scale of the surface shown; it is always answered, and a refusal ends the request of the principal, so a later drag asks again. A release ends a drag of the principal in progress on that window with no `Resized`, and a drag that ends on a window that is released or has a surface pending asks for nothing (`bar.drag_ended_by_release`, `bar.drag_asks_size`, pure procedures with host tests): a request to a window between two surfaces would wait for an answer that the window cannot give. `Surface_Rescale` is taken only in Released, and is always answered (`bar.surface_rescale_refusal`, a pure procedure with host tests): a gift of this process with Read; no request of the principal and no pending surface; the serial one past the surface released; the scale of the compositor; its pixels exactly the logical size of the window at that scale; memory for two pictures of it. The pending surface keeps its logical size and its scale, so the `Present` that shows it computes nothing again. `Surface_Declined` is taken only in Released, with the serial one past the serial released and the scale of the compositor (`bar.surface_declined_refusal`, a pure procedure with host tests): the compositor moves the serial released to it, keeps the frame with the background, writes `Window_Declined_Scale`, and names the count of the windows that declined the scale in force in a `Settings_Changed` of the same scale to each window, which the window of settings shows. It sends no answer: the window is already in Released. A window that declined the scale in force counts as settled for the line `Windows_At_Scale`; it leaves the count when it offers a surface again or closes.

**Why these forms.**

- **Release first, then make the new surface.** A rescale holds one surface at a time, so the budget of a window, which counts its two pictures at the largest scale, 2, pays it. Holding both was refused: the window of 1000 × 704 would need 4 749 pages of pictures in place of 2 600, and the least budget of the windows would grow by about 2 100 pages each. The cost is the frames between the release and the first `Present` of the new surface, in which the compositor draws the frame of the window with the background.
- **A declined scale, not a stop.** A window whose budget cannot pay its new surface declines the scale: it holds no picture, the compositor draws its frame with the background, and it offers a surface again at the next scale that differs. The budget counts the pictures at 2, so only a budget that its starter limited (a disk process) or a window grown past its stated size meets this. The first form stopped the process, which closed a window of the principal for a choice of the scale. A window that kept its old scale was refused, as before: it needed a second unit for each place on the ring, and the pointer of the compositor would then reach another element than the one drawn under it.
- **An answer to each request, no replacement.** A newer `Surface_Rescale` that replaces a pending one leaves the window unsure of which surface the compositor holds; with one answer to each request and one request at a time, the two sides never disagree.
- **A surface never draws outside its window.** The compositor cuts the surface to the screen rectangle of the logical interior of the window, so the larger surface of the old scale, before its release, never covers the windows under it, and damages the screen rectangle of an old surface when a new one takes its place.
- **The background only where the surface leaves room.** A surface of the scale in force can be one pixel short of its interior on the screen, whose edges round on their own; the frame, which reaches one logical pixel under the interior, covers that pixel. A background under the whole interior would write each pixel of the window twice at each frame that redraws it. So the compositor fills only the strips at the right of a smaller surface and under it, where the frame does not reach (`bar.background_strips`, with a host test that no pixel is written twice and none is left): a surface of the scale in force leaves none.
- **The display presents the first picture of a rescale.** Right after `Surface_Rescale`, `display` fills the offered surface with the background and presents it (`rescale_first_picture`). The compositor takes the offer and shows that picture in the order of the ring, so a rescale ends for each window whose process reads its events, whether or not the process draws. The first form waited for a picture of the process: `remote` drew only after a served call, and the change of the scale never settled. The refused forms: a picture of the old surface scaled by the compositor, which the release of the old surface first leaves no pixel for; a rule for each process to draw at each answer, which the next process that draws on its own changes breaks again.
- **A window reads its events at each turn.** The scale reaches a window only through `Settings_Changed`, and the answers through `Surface_Answer`. A process that never reads them keeps its surface at the old scale, and the line `Windows_At_Scale` never comes. It also spins: `display.wait` ends at once while an unread record waits in the ring of the window. `workers` read none, so its loop drew with no pause from the first event that it got, and it never rescaled.
- **A window owes a picture of each new surface.** The compositor shows a surface from the first `Present` of its serial, so a window that holds a surface that it may draw, and presented none of it, owes a picture (`display.picture_owed`, a pure procedure `picture_owed_of` with host tests). The picture of the display above is no drawing of the process, so it leaves the picture owed. A process whose window draws only when its own state changes asks it at each turn and draws while it holds, so its content comes back after a rescale; `remote` and `terminal` draw by a flag of their own, and both ask it.
- **A line that a frozen window never writes.** The compositor writes a line at the first `Present` of each new serial, and one line after a change of the scale when each window shows a surface of the scale in force (`Windows_At_Scale`). The machine test waits for both: a window that stopped between two surfaces fails the step, the windows that the test does not name too.

**The limit of a window from outside the boot image.** The budget of a window counts its pictures at its stated size, at the largest scale. A resizable window whose principal grows it past that size pays the larger surface from its working memory; a disk process has a smaller largest budget and no exception. At a change of the scale such a window can fail to make its offer: it then declines the scale (above), and shows its frame until a scale that its budget pays. A window that states its largest size, as `clock` does, never meets this limit.

### When the compositor changes the window

- At `Surface_Resize`, the compositor decides with `bar.surface_resize_refusal`, a pure procedure with host tests: a gift of this process with Read, memory for two pictures, a resizable window, a request that waits, its asked size, and that size inside the limits. Then it maps the memory, the request ends, and the compositor answers `Resize_Taken`. Any other `Surface_Resize` is refused, counted and answered `Resize_Refused`, and the request ends; the size of the window does not change: a process cannot change the size of its window when the principal did not ask for it, and a second answer to one request is refused.
- `Resize_Declined` ends a request with no change. With no request, it is refused and counted.
- The old surface stays on the screen. The window changes at the first `Present` after `Surface_Resize`: its frame takes the new size and the edges opposite to the drag stay in place. Then the rule of the places (`bar.window_place_kept`) keeps a part of the title on the screen and under the bar, so the principal can always take the window and its close button. The old mapping is unmapped. The process lets its old surface go at `Resize_Taken`, so the old memory object ends when both sides let it go: the budget of the process pays for both surfaces between `Surface_Resize` and the later of the answer and the `Present`.
- The compositor takes the one answer of the one request, so the process and the compositor never disagree on the size, whatever the order of a drag, an answer and a `Present`.

### A process that does not handle `Resized`

It ignores the event. Its next `present` sends `Resize_Declined` before the picture, because `next_event` kept the request: the window keeps its old size and its old picture, and a later drag asks again. A process that never declares itself resizable never gets the event, and its edges give no resize shape.

## The refused forms

- **The compositor makes the new surface.** The compositor cannot pay with the budget of the process: it holds no budget handle of it, and a budget handle of each process in the compositor gives it a right that no other task needs. If it paid with its own budget, a process could make the compositor pay for any size.
- **A new surface at each step of the drag.** Each step costs a memory object and a whole frame of the process; a slow process then shows a size that the principal left. One size at the release costs one surface.
- **The old picture stretched or cut during the drag.** A stretched picture is a picture that the process did not draw, and a cut one hides a part of the content.
- **The compositor waits for the new picture before it draws again.** One process could then stop the desktop.
- **The asked size alone ties an answer to a request.** It did while a resize was the one request of a window. A rescale is a second kind of request, and its surfaces have the same logical size, so the surface serial names each surface and each answer.
- **Any answer within the limits.** A process could then grow its window when the principal did not ask, and with the edges of an old drag move it under the bar or off the screen.
- **A bound of time on a request.** See "One request at a time for each window".
- **A resizable window by default.** A window whose layout has fixed sizes would get a size that it cannot draw.
- **The minimum from the compositor alone.** Only the process knows the width of its widest text.

## Subtle points

- The process side refuses what the compositor refuses (another size, no request, a size outside the limits) before any kernel operation, so an honest process never makes a surface that the compositor refuses. A host test runs these refusals and `No_Slot` on a ring in plain memory; `No_Budget` comes from the kernel, and no test makes it yet.
- `Resizable`, `Surface_Resize` and `Resized` cross a border as words: the size limits as `u64`, read before any cast to `int`, so a word past the limit is refused and never becomes a negative size.
- The edges of a drag are a `bit_set` of an enum (`bar.Resize_Edges`), and a corner is two members. The pure procedures of the edges and the sizes are in `src/library/internal/compositor/bar/window_size.odin`, with host tests.
- The drag moves an edge by one logical pixel for each logical pixel of the pointer: the edge follows the pointer, and the size is the one that the principal drags, cut only by a limit or the screen ([`docs/DESIGN.md`](../../../docs/DESIGN.md), "6. Spacing"; [spacing_tokens.md](spacing_tokens.md)). The drag first moved by steps of 8, which the spacing tokens replaced.
- The budget of `clock` counts its window at its largest size, and its working memory holds a second surface at the largest size, for a resize between two large sizes, when both exist. A process whose budget counts its window at its first size gets `No_Budget` for a larger one: the designed refusal.
- The compositor maps the new surface beside the old one for the time between `Surface_Resize` and `Present`. Its budget counts the page tables of one such second mapping of the largest surface for each client (34 pages for 2816 × 2816 pixels of the screen, 1088 for 32 clients); the arithmetic is beside `MODULE_BUDGET` in `src/modules/system/compositor/main.odin`.
- The machine test reads the size from the compositor: `compositor: surface <title> <width>x<height> at <x>,<y>` is written again when the window takes the new surface.
