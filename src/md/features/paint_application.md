# The paint application

The process `paint` is a small paint program: brushes of several sizes, an eraser, a palette and a picker of any color, the tools line, rectangle, ellipse and fill, undo and redo, pictures that its private store keeps as they change, and a status line. It is also a service: the console draws on its picture with isolated calls (`paint.line`, `paint.write`), which shows that one process is an application and a service with one thread.

## Constraints

- One thread and no lock: a call of the console runs between two events of the window.
- The window is resizable ([window_resize.md](window_resize.md)). A resize must not lose a pixel of the picture.
- The drawing module draws one picture memory for a window: the picture is that memory.
- The process keeps its pictures in its private store (grant `Store`); no other process reads them.
- Each table has a stated capacity, and the history the bound of one copy of the picture; a refusal is said, never silent.
- Rectangular forms; no rounded blobs.

## The chosen form

- **The picture has its own size.** Its extent is its size: its pixels are rows of its width, in memory of the size of the picture ("The memory of a picture"). A larger window grows the extent with paper; a smaller window shows the top left part and keeps the rest. So a resize never cuts the picture, and a coordinate of the picture never moves. A wider extent moves each row to the new width, the last row first, so a row never covers one that is not moved yet. The export `extend` grows the picture past the window, to a photo of 6000 × 4000 or a picture of 4096 × 4096; the window shows its top left part.
- **The layout**: a row of tools at the top (brush, eraser, line, rectangle, ellipse, fill, then smaller and larger for the size), a row of the palette under it (16 colors, the custom color when it is no color of the palette, and the button of the custom color), the canvas, and the status line at the bottom: the tool, the size, the color in hexadecimal, the place of the pointer on the picture, and the last message. The tool row says the tool by its chosen button only, and the size by no number, so the status line is the one place of each value. The action bar is at the right of the status line: undo, redo, pictures, clear.
- **The keys**: B brush, E eraser, L line, R rectangle, O ellipse, F fill, [ and ] the size, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y redo, Ctrl+O the panel of the pictures, C clear. The right button draws with the paper color, as the eraser does.
- **The sizes** are 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 and 34 pixels: each step is visible, and the list is short.
- **The shapes** show a preview while the button is held, as records of the draw table over the picture, and are drawn into the picture at the release. The ellipse is drawn by spans: for each row, the columns between the inner and the outer ellipse. A preview of an ellipse is a path of 64 points, at angles in turns.
- **The fill** is a scanline fill of spans of the 4-connected region of one color. Each entry of its stack is a run that it painted, with the row next to it to read; a pop reads that row under the run, paints each run of the region that it finds in one pass, and pushes it, with the parts of the run that pass the run before it, which the row of that run must read too. A painted pixel has the new color, so it is never read as the region again: no bitmap of the reached pixels, no queue of pixels, no recursion. The stack is in its own arena of the budget, which grows as the fill needs (`fill_stack_grow`). A fill whose stack the budget does not pay stops: the window puts the part that it painted back from the copy, and the status line says it. Measures on a photo of 6000 × 4000 (an optimized build, a host with a load of 9 to 12): a fill of a sky of 22.8 million pixels took 69 ms with the fill of pixels and its bitmap, and takes 18 to 20 ms with 5 spans at most; a maze of that size (a wall at each odd pixel of each odd row), the worst case, takes 67 to 101 ms and 5 995 004 spans (96 MB, a copy of the picture).
- **A long change runs on a worker.** A fill learns the size of its region as it runs, the record of a large step compresses its whole box, an undo or a redo of it puts the whole box back, and a clear writes the whole picture: on the largest picture (2^28 pixels) each passes one frame by far. So each fill, and each record, undo, redo and clear whose box passes `LONG_CHANGE_PIXELS` (262 144 pixels, under half a millisecond), runs on a second worker thread of the window, the worker of the changes. The window draws a frame at least each 100 ms while it runs, and its status line gives the progress (the pixels painted, or the part of the step of undo kept, undone or redone) and "Escape cancels". Escape sets the cancel of the fill and of the history (atomic words), which each reads at each span or strip. A fill, a clear or a record that stops is put back at the take from the copy before the change, and the history keeps no step of it. An undo or a redo that stops in its check has written nothing; in its write, it keeps each strip in the copy before a change before it writes it (that copy is free: no change runs), and puts the strips back; a step held in the copy is an exchange, its own inverse, so it exchanges back the rows that it did. While a long change runs, nothing else reads or writes the picture, its copy, the history or the stack of the fill: a stroke, an undo, a clear, a growth of the picture, the store and the calls of the console wait, and the progress line says why. The store has its own worker, so a keep never delays a change.
- **The copy before a change** is made tile by tile, at the first write (`Copy_Before`): a change points the picture to it (`change_begin`), and each tool gives it the box that it writes next, so it copies each tile of 64 × 64 pixels that it writes once, and marks it in a bitmap of the tiles (8.3 KiB for the largest picture). A segment gives the pieces of one tile side along it, a rectangle its four borders, an ellipse the runs of each row, a fill each run. At the end of the change, the tiles of its box that it did not write are copied from the picture, whose pixels they still are, then the record reads the copy; the marks are reset (`change_finish`). So the start of a stroke copies nothing, and a stroke copies the tiles that it writes: on 6000 × 4000, the start of a stroke of 21 pixels took 8.4 to 8.7 ms on the window with the copy of the whole picture, and takes 6 to 8 µs with the copy of its one tile (an optimized build). A cancel puts back the copied tiles of the box, the only ones that the change wrote. A call of the console during a stroke writes through the same copy, so the step of the stroke holds it, as a step held what a call drew in its box before.
- **Undo and redo** keep, for each change, the rectangle that it changed (the box of the stroke, the shape or the fill, never the whole picture), with its pixels before and after, compressed with LZ4 (`modus:contract/lz4`) by strips of the whole rows of the box that fit `HISTORY_STRIP_PIXELS` (65536) pixels, at least one row. A strip is the scratch of a step, never a whole picture, and a step is checked whole before an undo writes a pixel. The box of a step names rows and columns, so an undo after the picture grew wider writes the same pixels in the rows of the new width. A new step is measured once (each strip compressed, its bytes summed, the measure stopped at the bound); the oldest steps go until it fits; then it is written: no compression again for each step that goes. A new change after an undo removes the changes that redo would give. The bound of the history is its memory, not a count of steps ("The memory of the history").
- **The custom color**: a sample of the color, three channels, red, green and blue, each a bar where a press or a drag sets the value, with buttons -16 and +16, and a field for `#RRGGBB` with the button apply.
- **No save action** (property 9, "Persistence: no save action"). The picture in the window is always a picture of the store: the store keeps it as it changes, and each keep is a version that the store keeps by its retention rule. The principal never saves, and never loses a picture by a forgotten act.
- **No change before the first open.** From the start to the take of the first open (the list, and the read of the newest picture), the picture does not change: a stroke is refused, the status says that the store opens, and the calls of the console wait in their ring. A stroke then would be replaced by the picture that the open reads.
- **The start opens the picture of the newest change.** At the start, the window lists the store and opens the picture whose last commit is the newest, so a stop and a start of the process give back the drawing. A store with no picture starts a new one with the first name `picture-1`, `picture-2`, ... that the store does not hold; a new picture is written at its first change, so a picture that nobody touched leaves no object.
- **The list pages.** The panel shows one page of the names of the store: the pictures of one page of `storage.list` (`LIST_ROWS`, 64 objects), with the buttons "previous" and "next". A page is the unit of the list, never the store: the window holds no name past its page, so no count of pictures bounds the store. "next" lists after the last object of the page; "previous" lists from the start to the page before (one call for each page before it, on the worker thread), so the window keeps no cursor of each page. The open of the store reads each page once, to the end: it gives the first page, the picture with the newest change, and the names `picture-N` that are taken. A list that failed gives no name, and the status says that the picture is not kept.
- **A name is free when the store says so.** A new picture, and a rename of a picture that the store does not hold yet, ask the worker whether the store holds the object of the name (`storage.size`): the page of the window cannot say it. A new picture waits for the answer; if the open picture changed while the answer came, the act waits for its keep and asks again. A rename of a picture that the store holds asks no question: the store refuses a rename to a name that exists.
- **The keep is coalesced.** Each change of the picture (a stroke, a shape, a fill, an undo, a clear, a call of the console, a taller extent) adds its rows to a band of changed rows. A keep starts when the picture has not changed for `KEEP_PAUSE_MILLISECONDS` (1 second), or `KEEP_LONGEST_WAIT_MILLISECONDS` (5 seconds) after the first change that the band holds, whichever comes first, and before another picture opens or a new one starts. Without the second bound, a principal who draws without a pause of a second would never be kept. A keep is one commit for a band of rows under `KEEP_COMMIT_BYTES`: one version for a pause of the principal, not one for each point of a stroke.
- **Each committed state is one whole picture** (property 9: never half written). A keep that fits one commit (a band of at most `KEEP_COMMIT_BYTES`, 1 MiB, of rows) writes its rows and the header and commits once: the commit is atomic. A keep that does not fit one commit cannot be one commit: a call carries 64 KiB, and the store holds 4 MiB of pending changes for all its connections. The store has no atomic replace: `rename` refuses a name that exists. So the header names the place of the rows of the picture in the object:
  - A keep that cannot be one commit, or of a picture whose width changed (each row moves), writes the whole picture at another place, in pieces, with a commit after each `KEEP_COMMIT_BYTES`: the header still names the old place, so each of these commits holds the old picture whole. Its last commit writes the header that names the new place: one write of 24 bytes, so the commit before it holds the old picture and the commit itself holds the new one.
  - The other place is just after the header when the new rows fit before the old ones, and just after the old rows else; never over them. A keep before the old rows sets the size of the object to the end of the new rows in its last commit, which drops the old rows. So the rows start at most two pictures of the largest size past the header (`FILE_ROWS_OFFSET_LIMIT`), and the place goes back to the start at the next large keep of the same size.
  - The first keep of a new picture that does not fit one commit writes a temporary object `<name>.partial`, which the list ignores, then `rename` makes it `<name>.picture` in one commit: the name does not exist yet, so the rename takes it.
  - A stop before that rename leaves a `<name>.partial` that no picture names. The open of the store removes each one that it lists, before any keep can write one: a later first keep of the same name then starts from no object.
  - A keep copies the rows of the window piece by piece, when it asks for each piece. A stroke that the principal draws between two pieces of a large keep reaches the rows of the later pieces and not of the earlier ones. The commit then holds a whole valid picture that is neither the picture before the stroke nor the picture after it. The rows of the stroke are in the band of the next keep, which starts at most 5 seconds later and makes the store equal to the window. Copying the whole picture before the keep would need a second picture of memory, which the process cap does not hold.
  - A host test runs each keep on a store in memory and reads the picture after each commit: each read gives the old picture or the new one when no stroke comes during the keep, and a valid picture when one comes; after the next keep, the store equals the window. A stop before the rename leaves a temporary object, which the open removes.
- **The keep writes the changed rows.** A row of the picture is at one place of the file while the width stays. A keep writes the header and the band of changed rows with `storage.write_range`, sets the size of the object, and commits: the store hashes and writes only the blocks of those rows. The first keep of a new picture writes all its rows. Two bounds of the store shape it: a call carries at most 64 KiB (the call region of a connection), so the rows go in pieces of `KEEP_PIECE_BYTES` (32 KiB), and a read takes the size of the object, then the object in pieces of the same size; and the store holds at most 4 MiB of pending changes for all its connections, so a keep commits after each `KEEP_COMMIT_BYTES` (1 MiB) of rows. A keep of a few rows is one commit; the first keep of a photo of 6000 × 4000 (96 MB) is 92.
- **The pictures panel** opens at the right of the canvas (Ctrl+O, or the action pictures): a field for a name with the buttons "new" (a new picture with that name) and "rename" (the open picture takes that name), the list of the pictures of the store, and the buttons "open" and "delete". The open picture cannot be deleted. The status line names the open picture. A name has 1 to `NAME_LIMIT` characters (the longest name of an object of the store, `store.ENTRY_NAME_CAPACITY`, less the suffix `.picture`: a name follows the names of the store, and a longer store name gives a longer picture name) among the lowercase letters, the digits, `-` and `_`. A picture is the object `<name>.picture` of the private store. The canvas gets narrower; the picture keeps its extent.
- **The picture file**: a header of 24 bytes (the magic `MPIC`, then the version 4, the width, the height, each a little-endian u32, and the place of the rows, a little-endian u64), then the rows at that place: `height` rows of `width` pixels of 4 bytes. A reader reads the header, then the rows only. It checks each field before it uses it: a width or a height of 0, past `PICTURE_SIDE_LIMIT` (65536) or past `PICTURE_PIXELS_LIMIT` pixels together, a place in the header, past `FILE_ROWS_OFFSET_LIMIT` or not a multiple of 4, an object too short for its rows, a pixel whose top byte is not 0, and a picture that the memory of the window does not hold are refused, and the picture does not change.
- **The exports** keep their contract on the picture: `line` and `write` take a color of the palette (0 to 15) and coordinates of the picture; `write` refuses a place outside the extent. `clear` and `lines` are as before. A call is a change of the picture with no step in the history: an undo after a call can paint over what the call drew, in the box of its step. A step for each call would let a caller fill the history of the principal.
- **The store runs on the worker thread** of the window ([background_work.md](background_work.md)): the open of the store, the keep, the read, the new picture, the rename, the delete and the list are jobs, one at a time. A keep is a series of jobs of one piece each: the window copies the header and the rows of the next piece into the buffer of the job before each ask, so no copy of the whole picture exists. A read puts the rows in the array of the copy before a stroke, which is free from the ask of a read to its take, and the window decodes them after the take. The principal draws on while a keep runs.
- **The keep is a state machine of the model** (`keep_step`, `keep_wake`, `keep_refused`, `pending_ask`): a turn of the loop gives it the facts (the store, the name, a job that runs, a drag) and it gives the job to ask and the time to wake. Its rules:
  - The loop wakes for a keep only when a turn can then ask for it: never with no store, for a picture with no name, or while a job runs (its end wakes the loop).
  - A read, a new picture and a rename wait for the keep of the open picture; a new picture waits for the end of a stroke too. A delete waits for no keep: when the store is full, the delete is what makes room.
  - A keep that the store refused puts its rows back, drops the act that waited for it (the status says so), and the next try waits `KEEP_RETRY_MILLISECONDS` (5 seconds): no loop of tries.
  - From the ask of a read to its take, the picture does not change: a stroke, an undo, a clear and the calls of the console wait or are refused, and the status says that a picture is read.
- **The memory of the history.** The steps are a ring of bytes in a fourth arena of the budget, which grows up to one copy of the memory of the picture (its width × its height × 4 bytes) and no further. Each step is its head (its box, the bytes of its two parts, the places of the step before and after it) and its two parts, one run of bytes: so the history has no table of steps, and no count of steps bounds it. A new step goes after the newest; at the end of the bound it goes to the start of the ring; the oldest steps go until it has its room. A drop moves no byte: a ring, not a copy of the steps that stay to the start, which cost a move of up to one picture after each stroke. A budget that does not pay the growth bounds the ring at what it holds, as a smaller bound.
- **A step larger than the bound** is kept as the one step of the history, in the copy before the stroke, which exists already: the history drops each other step and keeps the box. An undo or a redo of it exchanges the box between the picture and the copy, so the copy holds the other state. The copy serves again at the next stroke, at a picture that gets wider (its rows move) and at a read (its rows are written there): the history drops that step first. So the largest change undoes, at no memory past the copy, and only once.
- **The memory of a picture.** The pixels of the picture, its copy before a stroke, the stack of a fill and the history are four arenas of the budget of `paint` ([memory_without_a_bound.md](memory_without_a_bound.md)), not pages of the module. At its start, `paint` reserves the pages that its budget holds for each (`system.arena_reservation_pages` of `Budget_Read`), and each arena grows by a push at its end, so its memory is one run from a base that never moves: the picture memory of the draw tables stays valid. A growth of the picture grows the arenas of the picture and of its copy first (the history grows at a record, and the stack of a fill as the fill needs); a budget that does not pay them refuses the growth, the picture stays as it was, the status line says it, and the log has `Picture_Memory_Refused`. A read grows the arena of the copy to the rows of the file on the worker thread, so the window grows no arena while a read runs, and a growth waits too while a stroke (its copy has the width of before) or a keep (its pieces are places in the picture) runs. The bounds of a picture are bounds of one memory object, not a size of a screen: `PICTURE_PIXELS_LIMIT` (2^28 pixels, 1 GiB) and `PICTURE_SIDE_LIMIT` (65536, so that a strip of the history holds one row). The module budget states the picture of a photo of 24 MP (6000 × 4000): three arenas of 23 438 pages (the picture, its copy and the history at its bound), a stack of a fill of 733 pages, the frame lists of the four reservations and the stacks of two worker threads, 77 935 pages in all with the window; a compile-time check ties the literal to these parts. The root service gives at most its largest budget: a smaller budget holds a smaller picture, refused as a value, never a stop. The window and the second surface that a resize holds in the working memory are counted by the rule of the tool: `paint` is resizable, and its budget states its least size, 600 × 360, the constants that it passes to `display.resizable_set`; that size fits the logical screen at the largest scale, so each picture at the largest window at that scale is cut on each axis to the reference screen, 1920 × 1080 pixels, 4 050 pages for two pictures ([desktop_to_browser.md](desktop_to_browser.md), "The budgets"). The check writes that rule, its condition too, from `scale_factor.LARGEST`, `scale_factor.LARGEST_LOGICAL_WIDTH`, `scale_factor.LARGEST_LOGICAL_HEIGHT` and `reference_screen`, never with a scale written by hand: a check at 11/8 by hand passed when the largest scale became 2, and paint then asked for 20 699 pages. The tool reads the working memory as a literal, so a second check ties that literal to its parts.
- **The pure parts** (the picture operations, the fill, the ellipse, the history, the file format, the name rule) are in `internal:paint/paint_model`, with host tests.

## Refused forms

- **A picture of the size of the window.** A smaller window would cut the picture, and a larger one would need a copy at each step of the drag.
- **A whole copy of the picture for each step of the history.** A photo of 24 MP is 96 MB: 32 steps would need 3 GB.
- **A count of steps** (64 before). A count bounds the small strokes, which cost little, and not the large ones, which cost much: the bytes are the cost, so the bytes are the bound.
- **A step larger than the bound refused** (the step could not be undone, as before). The copy before the stroke holds its pixels already: a refusal threw away an undo that costs nothing.
- **The names of the whole store in the window** (64 before). A store has no count of objects, so the window would need a list that grows to it, for a panel that shows one page; and the check of a free name in that list can be old: the store answers it.
- **A buffer of the whole file.** 96 MB more for a keep and a read of a photo; the calls of the store move 32 KiB at a time anyway.
- **A picture of a fixed largest size in the pages of the module.** The module paid the largest picture before the first stroke, twice, and a photo of a phone did not fit the largest budget of a process. A picture in arenas pays what it is.
- **Two slots of rows of the largest picture in the file.** A slot of a fixed row and height cannot hold a picture of any size; the place of the rows in the header holds each.
- **A recursive fill.** The stack of the thread is 64 pages.
- **A fill of pixels with a bitmap of the visited ones** (before). It read each pixel three to four times through the bitmap, and its stack of fixed size made a scan of the whole region when it was full: 69 ms on a sky of 22.8 million pixels, against 18 to 20 ms for the spans.
- **A copy of the whole picture at the start of each stroke** (before). It cost a copy of the picture on the window at each press: 96 MB for the stated photo, 1 GiB for the largest picture.
- **A bitmap of rows** for the copy before a change. A row is the width of the picture: a vertical segment, or the sides of a rectangle, write each row of their box, so whole rows copied the whole picture on the window, 70 MB for a segment across the canvas on a picture of 16384 × 16384. A tile bounds the copy by the area written.
- **A long change on the window.** A fill of a photo, or the record of its step, stalled the window for tens of milliseconds, and many more on a larger picture; a window must not stall.
- **A long change in steps between the frames of the window.** A fill cut into slices of a few milliseconds keeps one thread, but each slice must stop and resume its stack, the window waits for each slice anyway, and the record is one compression that is not cut. A worker thread costs one stack of 48 pages.
- **A picker by hue and value in a square.** It needs a conversion of colors that a principal does not read; three channels give each color with exact values.
- **A save action** (a button "save", a key Ctrl+S). Property 9 says that the system has no save action: a document in the store is always current, and a process sends each change. A save would make the store hold an older picture than the window until the principal acts.
- **A write of the whole object at each keep.** Property 9 asks the store for "apply this change atomically", not "replace the whole object". A photo of 24 MP is 96 MB; a stroke changes a few rows.
- **A temporary object for each large keep, renamed over the picture.** The store refuses a rename to a name that exists, and a remove before the rename leaves a commit with no picture. The places of the rows keep one object, with one history of versions.
- **A commit for each piece.** A band of old rows written over in several commits mixes old and new rows in the commits between.
- **A compressed file (LZ4).** A change of one row changes the bytes after it: no range of the file stays in place, so each keep would write the whole object.
- **A keep at each change.** A stroke is a change at each event of the pointer: one version for each point would fill the versions of the store with steps that no principal asks for.
- **A file format of another system (PNG).** It needs a deflate coder and a CRC, and it is compressed: see above.

## Subtle points

- While a fill runs on the worker, the drawing module can read pixels of the picture that the fill writes, for a part of the canvas that a menu or a move damaged: an aligned word of 4 bytes reads whole, so it reads the old or the new color of a pixel, never a mix. The generation of the picture changes only on the window, at the take, so the drawing module draws the whole result then.
- A cancel ends with the change that it asked: the take clears it, so a record of a small change on the window after it is not cancelled.

- The history is cleared when a picture is opened or a new one starts: an undo across two pictures has no meaning.
- The bound of the history is read at each record, from the picture of that time: a picture that grew gives a larger ring. The extent never shrinks while the history lives, so the bound never falls under the bytes that the ring holds.
- A picture that a job read is taken only after the drag that runs ends: the drag holds the copy before its change for its step of undo, and a step of the old picture over the new one would paint old pixels into it. Undo, redo and clear are refused during a drag for the same reason.
- The cost of the pause: a stop of the process loses the changes since its last pause of one second. Property 9 names that the change in progress of a process that keeps by pause ([`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "Persistence: no save action, and versions for all processes"), so the guarantee holds.
- A drag that starts on the canvas keeps drawing when the pointer leaves it: the points are cut to the extent, and the release ends the change.
- A change of the picture raises the generation of the Picture record, so the drawing module draws it again; the preview of a shape is records only, so it does not touch the picture until the release.
