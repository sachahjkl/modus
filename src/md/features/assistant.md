# The assistant

A principal asks an agent for help from the desktop. The application `assistant` is a window where the principal types a request and reads the conversation: the replies of the agent, each act that the agent proposes, and its result. The agent itself, the agent runner, runs on the host: it reads each request through `remote`, asks a language model, and runs each proposed act only through the host agent bridge ([agent_bridge.md](agent_bridge.md)). The bridge stops each act that changes a grant or that is not on its read-only list, and the principal decides such an act on the trusted display only.

The terms Assistant, Agent runner, Model reply and Act dialog are in [`docs/glossary/access.md`](../../../docs/glossary/access.md). The guide is [`docs/ASSISTANT.md`](../../../docs/ASSISTANT.md).

## Purpose

- Give a principal an agent from the desktop, with no terminal and no second program on the host to watch.
- Show the principal each act that the agent proposes before its result, and the result after it, in one conversation.
- Keep each guarantee of the bridge: an act of the agent runs only through a capability of the bridge, a grant-adding or sensitive act stops with `Awaiting_Principal_Approval`, and the agent never answers a trusted surface.
- Keep the model, its endpoint and its key outside the system.

## Constraints

- [`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "19. A command is a function: no text between processes". The window never parses a text of another process. The request, the reply and the result text are words for a person; they cross as Text fields that the window shows. Each fact that the window acts on is a typed value: a serial, an act number, an act state, the words of an error record.
- [`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "20. No ambient access: the other holes to close". The window draws no false system dialog: the act dialog is drawn by the trusted display, and shows names that the trusted display checks, never a word of the model.
- [`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "21. Principals: who acts, without users". The principal is the one source of a new grant. No act of the agent, no reply of the model and no call of the runner answers the act dialog.
- [`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "2. Sandbox by default". The assistant holds a window, its exports, and a connection to the trusted display with the right to ask an act: no store, no network, no machine, and no grant to act for the principal.
- [`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "24. Every input of a process passes through a channel that can be recorded". A request comes from the keys of the window; a reply and an act come through calls of `remote`, each a record of its connection.
- The tool uses the Odin standard library only, and no secret is in the repository, in the system, or in a log.

## The chosen form

### Four parties

1. **The window**, the module `assistant` of the group `applications`, category `Application`. It is a window and a service in one process with one thread, as `paint`: its exports run between two events.
2. **The agent runner**, `modustool assistant` on the host. It calls the exports of the window through `remote` with a client key that the principal allowed ([remote_client_keys.md](remote_client_keys.md)).
3. **The model**: a language model behind an HTTP endpoint of the host, or the fake model of the tests, a script of replies.
4. **The bridge**: the host agent bridge, which runs each act of a model reply, with its authorization, its read-only list and its act records.

### The flow of one request

1. The principal types a request and presses Enter. The window keeps it with a serial, from 1.
2. The runner calls `assistant.request_serial()`: the serial of the oldest request that no runner took, or 0. Then `assistant.request_text(serial)`, which marks the request taken.
3. The runner runs an agent loop for the request ("The agent loop"). At each step it gives the conversation to the model and reads its reply at the border of the model reply ("The model reply"). A refused reply goes to the window with `assistant.reply_refused(serial, …)`: the words of its error record. It ends the loop: no act of it comes.
4. A reply that passes: its text goes to the window with `assistant.reply(serial, text, acts)`, where `acts` is the count of its acts, 0 to 16. Each of its acts: `assistant.act(serial, kind, line, service, export)` gives the act number; the runner then gives the act to the bridge, and gives the outcome to the window with `assistant.act_result(act, state, text)`. After the last act that the step counted, the window waits for the next step; it refuses an act past the count with `State`. A reply with 0 acts is the final reply, and ends the loop.
5. An act that the bridge stops with `Awaiting_Principal_Approval` has the state `Awaiting_Principal`. The window then asks the trusted display for the act dialog, and shows the decision of the principal when the trusted display gives it. If the trusted display refuses the dialog for a cause other than `Busy`, or the window has no connection to it, the act takes the final state `Not_Shown` and nothing runs: the window never asks again for an act that the trusted display cannot show.

### The exports of the window

Each export refuses a caller that does not act for the principal with `No_Grant`. The serials, the act numbers and the states are checked at the border of the export: a serial that no request has, an act number that no act has, a state word that names no member of `assistant.Act_State`, a text past its bound, a text that is not UTF-8 with no control byte, and a name that is not an identifier are refused with their error record, and change nothing.

| Export | What it does |
|---|---|
| `request_serial() -> Integer` | The serial of the oldest request not taken, or 0 |
| `request_text(serial) -> Text` | The text of the request, and marks it taken; a result too small for the text is refused and takes nothing |
| `reply(serial, text, acts) -> Integer` | The text of one step of a taken request, and the count of its acts; 0 acts is the final reply. At most `AGENT_LOOP_STEP_CAPACITY` (16) for each request |
| `reply_refused(serial, kind, unit, argument, value, limit) -> Integer` | A refused model reply: the words of its error record; it ends the loop |
| `stop_requested(serial) -> Integer` | 1 when the principal pressed Stop or Escape on the running loop of the request, else 0. The runner reads it before each step and each act |
| `loop_end(serial, cause) -> Integer` | Ends the loop of the request with a word of `Agent_Loop_End`, which the window shows once; refused with `State` when the loop ended already |
| `act(serial, kind, line, service, export) -> Integer` | A proposed act of the reply, with its `Act_Kind` and the names of its one call; gives the act number |
| `act_result(act, state, text) -> Integer` | The outcome of the bridge for the act: `Done` with its result text, `Refused` with the refusal of the bridge, or `Awaiting_Principal` |
| `act_table(act, shape, name, total_rows, columns, cells) -> Integer` | The typed result of an act that is `Done` and gave a table or a record: its columns and its first rows; once for each act |
| `act_table_rows(act, cells) -> Integer` | More rows of a table that `act_table` started, so a wide table of 64 rows crosses the call region of 64 KiB in batches |
| `act_state(act) -> Integer` | The state of an act, which the runner reads after the decision of the principal |

The records, the enums and the bounds are the contract package `modus:contract/assistant`: a request of 4096 bytes at most (room for a pasted error or code), a reply and a result of 16 KiB at most, an act line of 200 bytes at most (the line of the bridge), 16 acts of one step, 16 steps of one request, 288 entries in the conversation of one run of the window: one whole loop of 16 steps of 16 acts and its request take 274. The window keeps the typed tables of the last `ACT_TABLE_POOL_CAPACITY` (16) acts; an older act keeps its words.

**The names of an act are typed values of the runner.** The act dialog names the act as `service.export`. The runner takes the two names from the line with `lex_line_single_call` of the bridge contract, the parser that the bridge itself uses, and sends them as two Text arguments of `act`. The window checks that each is an identifier and never cuts the line itself (property 19). A line that is no single call, or whose names are no identifiers of the system (more than 31 bytes, an upper case letter, a leading `_`), has no names: the runner sends two empty texts. The bridge can still stop such a line for the principal, but the act dialog cannot show it: the runner then writes `Refused` with "the act names no export that the trusted display can show", and `act_result` refuses the state `Awaiting_Principal` for an act with no names.

### The result of an act: a table, not a paragraph

The bridge gives the result of a call as a typed value: a value, or a record or a table with the name of its record, its columns and its rows. Each column has its kind, a member of `agent_bridge.Lex_Column_Kind` (text, integer, size, time, hexadecimal), and a unit word: the unit of a size or a time, the bytes of a hexadecimal number. A cell of a text column is its text; a cell of a number column is its value. The kind is what the drawers of the console use to align and write a value: a number at the right of its column, a size or a time with its unit in the head, "resident (pages)", a hexadecimal number in two digits for each byte. A form of a field with no kind of its own (a status name, a hash, a fixed point, a handle) is a text. The runner carries that form to the window, and the window draws it as the console draws a result: a table with a line of heads and aligned columns, a record as one line for each field with its name and its value, the first rows, then "N rows more are not shown" and "N rows of <record>". The drawers are the ones of the console, `modus:console_results`, which moved from the internal parts of the console to the public library because two modules draw with them.

- **The typed form crosses one export.** `act_table(act, shape, name, total_rows, columns, cells)` takes two tables to read: the columns, each a name, a kind word and a unit word (`Act_Table_Column`), and the cells, row after row, each a text and a value (`Act_Table_Cell`): a text column fills the text, a number column the value, and the other field is zero, so each fact of a cell is in one place. `shape` is a word of `Act_Table_Shape`: a table, or a record of one row.
- **The bounds.** At most `ACT_TABLE_COLUMN_CAPACITY` (24) columns, `ACT_TABLE_ROW_CAPACITY` (64) rows, the row bound of a step of the model, cells of `ACT_TABLE_CELL_BYTES` (64: a hash in hexadecimal), names of `ACT_TABLE_NAME_BYTES` (32). `total_rows` is the count of rows of the whole result: the runner carries the first 64, and the drawer writes the rest as a count. One remote call carries at most `ACT_TABLE_CELL_BATCH_CAPACITY` (512) cells: `act_table` takes the first whole rows, and `act_table_rows` the next ones, with the same checks of each cell. The window pages through the rows that it carries with "Previous rows" and "Next rows".
- **The border.** `act_table` refuses, whole and with its error record, each form that is not one: an act that is not `Done` or that has its table already (`State`), a shape that names no member, a record with another count of rows than 1, no column or more than 24, a count of cells that is not a whole count of rows of the columns, more than 64 rows, a total under the rows given, a name that is empty, a kind word that names no member of `Lex_Column_Kind` (None too), a unit word that does not fit its kind (`lex_column_unit_valid`), a text in a cell of a number column or a value in a cell of a text column, and a name or a cell that is not UTF-8 with no control character (`text_encoding.character_in_text`: U+0000 to U+001F and U+007F to U+009F), or that has a byte after the zero that ends its text. It reads each record of the call region once, into one staging copy, and checks the copy. The two words of a column, its kind and its unit, become its form here and nowhere else: `conversation.Column_Form`, one typed value for each kind (a text, a plain number, a `Size_Unit`, a `Duration_Unit`, the bytes of a hexadecimal number). The cells, the drawer and the window read the form, never a word. Nothing of a refused table shows.
- **The runner keeps the bounds.** Each character that `character_in_text` refuses becomes a space, in a cell, a name and the words of a result alike. A cell past 64 bytes ends with the cut mark of the system and the count of its missing bytes. The kind of a column crosses the JSON of the bridge by its name, and its unit as a number: an unknown name, `none`, a unit that does not fit, or a row of values of another width, gives no table. A result of more than 24 columns, or whose answer of the bridge is not a whole table (a row of another width, a cell that is not a text), has no table: the act shows its words only. A table that the window still refuses is no failure of the turn: the act keeps its words, and the report of the turn says why the table is missing.
- **A row of the drawer holds 256 bytes.** The window writes the description of the record in the format of the module contract (`console_results.description_write`), whose record holds `module.RECORD_SIZE_LIMIT` (256) bytes. A number column is an Integer of 8 bytes, a hexadecimal one has its bytes. Each text column is a Text column as wide as its longest cell. The size of a row counts the padding that the layout of the record gives a number column, at a multiple of its own size, and the row at a multiple of its widest number. When a row passes 256 bytes, the widest text columns take a common bound, the largest whose row fits, and a cell past its column ends with the cut mark. The bound never goes under the room of one character and the whole mark (`result_lines.CELL_LEAST`, 22 bytes): when even that bound passes 256 bytes, the last columns are dropped, and a note says how many.
- **A row is one line.** A row that goes on in a second line under its first columns breaks each column for the eye. The pure package `internal:assistant/result_lines` describes only the columns that the width of the window in cells holds, from the first, each as wide as its head and its widest cell, with the gap of the console; `draw_table` or `draw_record` then writes the lines with no width, so the drawer never narrows a column and never breaks a row. A record has one field on each line: a text value is bounded to the cells after the widest name and its gaps, and ends with the cut mark there, so no value runs past the window. One note line names the columns that are not shown: "8 columns more: handles_peak, mappings, ...", their count and their names in their order as far as the width holds them, then "...". The console keeps its own path: it gives the drawer its width, and a long row goes on under its columns there.
- **The rows fit the room of the window.** The package fits the lines to the room that the window has for the entry: the most rows whose lines fit, so the note of the drawer counts the others against `total_rows`; when even the head does not fit, the first lines and one line that counts the lines not shown. A note is a line of its own. Past the lines that a result keeps (its head, 64 rows or 24 fields, and the notes), the last line says that the result is cut: no line is dropped in silence.
- **The lines are kept, not computed in each frame.** The table of a done act never changes, so the window keeps the lines of the last three results that it drew, each with the width and the room that it was fitted to, and fits a result again only when one of them changes.
- **One draw record for each run of one color.** The window paints the lines in the font of the cells, each part in the color of what it is, as the console does. The parts of one color in a row, with the blanks between them, are one record at the column of their first part. The records and the text of a frame are sized from the lines of a frame in cells and the runs of a line (a run for each column and one for the notes), so the worst frame fits.
- **The text stays.** `act_result` still gives the words of the result: the log fact of the act, and the outcome that the model reads (with the role `tool`). The window draws the table in place of those words when the table came.

### The model reply

The runner asks the model for one JSON object and reads it at one border, `model_reply_decode` of the tool, before any act runs:

```
{"text": "There are 12 processes.", "acts": [{"kind": "eval_lex", "line": "monitor.processes()"}]}
```

- `text` is a string of 1 to 16 KiB, UTF-8 with no control byte but the line end, which the runner writes as a space: a Text that crosses to the window has no control byte.
- `acts` is an optional array of 0 to 16 acts; 0 acts is the final reply of the loop. An act has `kind`, one of the names of `Act_Kind` (`eval_lex` today), and `line`, a string of 1 to 200 bytes.
- An unknown field, a missing field, a field of another type, a duplicated field, text after the object, or a reply that is not JSON is refused whole: no act of it runs and no text of it shows. The error record names the field: `Bad_Value`, `Too_Long`, `Empty` or `Absent`, with the unit `Bytes` or `Records`.
- The whole reply is at most 256 KiB (`MODEL_REPLY_BYTES`); the answer of the endpoint at most 512 KiB, which holds such a reply escaped in the JSON of the chat completions form.
- The line of an act then passes the border of the bridge: `lex_line_classify` and `bridge_authorize_command`.

### The agent loop

One request is an agent loop of the design of [agent_code_mode.md](agent_code_mode.md), first layer: each act is one call of the read-only list, through the bridge.

- **Discovery.** The instructions of the runner name no service. They give the form of a reply and tell the model to find the commands with `manual.modules()`, `manual.exports(name)` and `manual.signature(name, n)`, which are on the read-only list of the bridge. A new module with a described export is a new command of the agent with no change of the instructions.
- **A step.** The model replies with 0 to 16 acts. The runner shows the reply, runs each act through the bridge, shows each outcome and its table, and gives each outcome back to the model in the role `tool`.
- **The size that the model reads.** The words of one act give at most `MODEL_RESULT_ROW_CAPACITY` (64) rows and `MODEL_RESULT_COLUMN_CAPACITY` (24) columns, with the count of the others, and all the outcomes of one step hold at most `MODEL_STEP_RESULT_BYTES` (16 KiB): each act gets an equal part of what the acts before it left, and a cut outcome ends with the cut mark.
- **The end.** The loop ends at the first of these, each a member of `Agent_Loop_End`: a reply with no act (`Final_Reply`); `AGENT_LOOP_STEP_CAPACITY` (16) steps (`Step_Limit`), after which the model gets no reply more; a step whose acts are the acts of the step before, in order, and whose outcomes are the outcomes of the step before (`Repeated_Step`): a repeat is known only from its results, so its acts run once more, and they read only; the same acts with new results are no repeat, because the agent can wait for a state that moves; a stop of the principal (`Stopped_By_Principal`), which the runner reads before each step, after the answer of the model, and before each act after the first; a refused or missing reply (`Model_Failed`); a call to the window that failed (`Window_Failed`); a refusal of the window with `No_Grant`, a refused client key, or a refusal of the bridge for its permission or its grant (`Grant_Ended`). The window shows the cause once, as the last entry of the request.
- **The window during the loop.** While a loop runs, a Stop button stands beside the field of the request, and Escape does the same. Each step shows once: its reply with `step N`, then its acts, the line of each in the font of the cells and its result as a table. An act of an older step, or of an older request, folds its result to one button, "Show 12 rows of Process"; a press opens it, and "Hide result" folds it again. The acts of the latest step with acts stay open.

### The view of the conversation

The entries show in a view of their own between the top of the window and the field of the request: a view that scrolls, not the scroll of the window layer ([window_scroll.md](window_scroll.md)). That scroll moves the whole layout of a window larger than its surface; the conversation is a view inside a window that fits, above a field that stays in place, anchored on its newest entry.

- **The newest entry shows.** The view follows the end of the conversation until the principal moves it up with the wheel over the view, the scroll bar at its right (the one bar of the package, `interface.scroll_bar`), Page Up and Page Down (the height of the view less one line), or Control with Home or End. A view moved up keeps the entry at its top and the same pixel of it, while entries are added under it, while older entries leave the ring above it, and while an entry changes its height (`internal:assistant/conversation_view`, `settle`). A view moved to the end, and a new request, follow again. When the entry at the top leaves the ring, the view shows the oldest entry.
- **Each entry fits the view.** The words of an entry show as many lines as the view holds; past them a button says how many lines more there are, "Show 140 more lines", and a press shows every line that the layout keeps, `WORD_LINE_CAPACITY` (512); "Show fewer lines" goes back, and says how many lines past that bound do not show. A typed result fits the view as its rows do, with "Previous rows" and "Next rows". So every entry is reachable, and none is taller than the view unless the principal asked for it.
- **A frame draws only the lines of the view.** Each frame draws the label, the lines of code, the lines of words and the lines of a result that meet the view, cut to it, and a button only when it shows whole. The lines of a frame are at most the lines of the view, so the text and the records of a frame keep the bounds of `window_memory` whatever the entries hold.
- **No frame works in proportion to the conversation.** Each entry has a number that stays while older entries leave the ring, and a revision that grows at each change of what the window shows of it. The window lays out an entry (its lines, its buttons, its height) when its number, its revision, its fold, the room or a font changed, and the places of all the entries when the layout serial of the conversation changed: at a change of the data, never in a frame with no change. A frame finds the entries that it shows by a binary search of the places. The fold of an act takes constant time: the conversation keeps the latest step with acts of the newest request.

### The act dialog: a face of the trusted display for one act

An act that the bridge stops needs a decision of the principal on the trusted display. The trusted display has two dialogs today, and neither says what the principal decides here: the grant dialog of `powerbox_ask` gives Enter to Allow and gives no decision to the asker; the plan difference dialog names a plan and says "Accept plan". A dialog of the trusted path that names another thing than the decision misleads the principal, so the act dialog is a third face of the same dialog state:

- `compositor.act_ask(act)` shows "TRUSTED ACT", the asker, and the act. The asker is the name of the calling process, which the compositor reads from the caller of the connection: a process cannot name another asker. The act is `service.export`, two identifiers that the compositor checks: never the arguments, never a word of the model. It is drawn in the font of the cells, Paper Mono, as each text that a reader compares character by character ([`docs/FONTS.md`](../../../docs/FONTS.md), section 12.6).
- `compositor.act_poll(serial)` gives the decision: pending, allowed, refused or unknown, the members of `Plan_Display_Decision`. The decision goes to its asker only: the connection that asked, while it lasts. Another caller, a serial of a plan dialog, and the serial of a dialog that a later one replaced read unknown.
- `compositor.act_cancel(serial)` closes the act dialog of its asker with a refusal: the window calls it when the entry of the act leaves its ring, so the principal never decides an act that the window no longer shows.
- The trusted display closes an act dialog with a refusal when the connection of its asker ends, as when its process exits, or when its grant no longer names an asker of acts (`Act_Asker_Lost`).
- The act starts as the act of the plan difference dialog: the focus starts on Refuse, Enter acts on the button that has it, and Escape refuses at once. The act face is stricter on the focus. A key that moves the focus (Tab, an arrow) counts only after the arm time of 500 ms, and each move of the focus starts the arm time again: Tab then Enter at once never allows, as no key typed for a window allows. A press on the button that has no focus moves the focus only, under the same rule; a press on the button that has it acts after the arm time. One dialog of the trusted display shows at a time; the others refuse with `Busy`.

### The right to ask an act

The act dialog serves a caller with the right to ask an act, and a caller that acts for its principal. The assistant has no grant to act for the principal: with it, its connection to the trusted display would open each export that serves such a caller, `plan_diff_ask` with free text on the face of a plan, `revoke_grant`, `powerbox_ask`, the screen and the notifications. The right is the term `asks_acts` of the start grant set of the assistant. The root service writes it as the amount `plans_recipes.DISPLAY_AMOUNT_ACT_ASKER` of the grant of each connection of such a process to the holder of Display (`role_policy.display_caller_amount`), as it writes `DISPLAY_AMOUNT_PLANS_HOLDER` for the holder of Plans ([declarations.md](declarations.md), "The trusted display of a plan"). The display reads it with `plans_recipes.act_display_caller_allowed`. The amount opens `act_ask`, `act_poll` and `act_cancel` only: `plan_display_caller_allowed` reads the amount of the holder of Plans, a value that differs.

**An allowed act does not run yet.** The bridge never takes an approval from its caller ([agent_bridge.md](agent_bridge.md), "Refused forms"), and the runner is its caller. An allowed act runs only when the approval is bound to that one act and checked by a party that the agent cannot drive: an act record of a wide grant with the proof of the principal ([wide_grants.md](wide_grants.md)), run in an agent console. Until then the window shows "allowed; the act runs when the agent console of a wide grant runs it", and nothing runs. A refused act shows "refused by the principal", and nothing runs.

### The host configuration of the model

The runner reads two environment variables at its start, and nothing else:

- `MODUS_ASSISTANT_ENDPOINT`: `http://host:port/path` of a chat endpoint in the form of the OpenAI chat completions request. Plain HTTP only: the standard library of Odin has no TLS, so an endpoint on another machine is reached through a local proxy that the operator runs. A URL with `https`, with a user part, or with no port is refused at the start. The host must be a loopback address (`127.0.0.0/8`, `localhost`, `[::1]`): plain HTTP to another host sends the key in clear on the network. The option `--key-in-clear-off-host` names that risk and lifts the rule. A host with a byte that is not a letter, a digit, `.`, `-`, or the brackets and colons of an address of IPv6 is refused.
- `MODUS_ASSISTANT_KEY`, or the variable that `--key-variable NAME` names: the bearer key. A secret of the keyring of the host reaches the runner under its name as such a variable. The runner never prints it, never writes it to a file, and never sends it to the machine. A refusal names the variable, never its value. A key with a space or a control byte, a line end too, is refused: it would add a line to the head of the request.

One answer of the endpoint has one deadline of 120 seconds for the send and the receive together, and each send and each receive has the time that is left as its timeout. An endpoint that stalls is a `Model_Failed` turn: the window shows `No_Answer`. The connection to a loopback address is immediate; the connection to another host has the bound of the operating system, as the standard library gives no other.

### The conversation of the model

The runner keeps the conversation that the model reads: the last 16 requests with their replies and the outcomes of their acts; the oldest request leaves with all its turns. A request is a turn of the role `user`. A reply is a turn of the role `assistant` that holds its acts as `tool_calls`. The outcome of an act is a turn of the role `tool` with the identifier of its call: a result of the machine never speaks as the principal.

`--fake <file>` takes the replies from a script of the tests in place of the endpoint, and reads no variable.

## Refused forms

- **The model in the system.** The system has no TLS client and keeps no secret of a host, and a model would hold the key in a store of the principal. The runner keeps the model outside, as the bridge keeps the agent outside.
- **The window parses the model reply.** The window would read a text of another party and act on it (property 19). The runner decodes the reply on the host and sends typed values.
- **The runner sends its own calls through the bridge.** The calls of the runner to the window are fixed code of the tool, the same for each reply; the bridge stands between the model and the machine. A model reply never names an export of the window: an act line that names `assistant` is not on the read-only list, so the bridge stops it.
- **The grant dialog of `powerbox_ask` or the plan difference dialog for an act.** The first gives Enter to Allow and no decision to its asker; the second names a plan. A dialog that names another thing than the decision misleads the principal.
- **The result of an act as one paragraph of text.** "slot 1, name root, state waiting, ..." for 18 rows is not readable, and a window that cut it back into a table would parse a text of another party (property 19). The table crosses as typed columns and cells.
- **A drawer of tables of the window's own.** The console draws each table of the system; a second drawer would draw the same table another way. The window uses the drawers of the console.
- **The grant `Acts_For_Principal` for the assistant.** One line, and the window would open each export of the trusted display that serves the principal, a grant much wider than one dialog.
- **The window cuts `service.export` from the line.** The window would read a text of another party and act on it (property 19). The runner sends the two names, taken by the parser of the bridge.
- **An act that asks the trusted display again after a refusal that is not `Busy`.** The refusal stays: the act would ask ten times a second forever, and hold back each act after it.
- **The act line or the text of the model on the trusted display.** Such words are a claim of the agent; the trusted display shows the asker and two identifiers that it checks.
- **An allowed act that the runner runs.** It is the self-approval that the bridge refuses: the runner would carry the approval as a claim.
- **The key in a file of the repository, in the command line, or in the log.** A command line is visible to each process of the host; an environment variable of one process is not.
- **HTTPS through a TLS library of C.** The tool uses the Odin standard library only.

## Subtle points

- **The serial of a request is taken once.** Two runners do not answer one request: `request_text` marks it taken, and `request_serial` gives only a request not taken.
- **Code in the font of the cells.** The window draws the line of an act and the result of an act that ran in Paper Mono, and the words that explain in B612: in B612 a parenthesis at 14 px reads as a bracket ([`docs/FONTS.md`](../../../docs/FONTS.md), section 12.6).
- **The window keeps 288 entries.** A request, a step reply, a refusal, an act and a loop end each take one; past 288 the oldest entry leaves the window, and a request whose entries left gets no new act: `act` refuses its serial with `Absent`. An act whose dialog shows when its entry leaves cancels that dialog with `act_cancel`.
- **The window bounds the acts of a step and the steps of a loop.** `reply` counts the acts, at most `MODEL_ACT_CAPACITY` (16); `act` refuses an act past that count, an act before the reply, and an act after a refused reply or after the end of the loop, each with `State`. A `reply` while acts of the step before are still due, and a `reply` or `reply_refused` after the end of the loop, are refused with `State`; a 17th step is refused with `Too_Long`.
- **A stop is a fact of the window, not of the runner.** Stop and Escape set a flag of the running request, which only the window writes; the runner reads it through `stop_requested` and ends the loop with `loop_end`. A runner that stops reading it cannot keep the window from showing that the principal asked to stop.
- **The text of a result is the text of the tool.** The runner writes the typed result of the bridge as words for the principal, at most 16 KiB, with the cut mark of the system, "... and N bytes more", past them. The window draws the table of a result in place of these words ("The result of an act").
- **One act dialog at a time.** A second act that waits for the principal shows its dialog after the first one is decided; until then it shows "waiting for the trusted display". Only `Busy` makes it wait: each other refusal is final.
- **A machine test refuses only.** The step `Key_Again_Until` presses a key until a fact shows. It takes only `ret` and `esc`, which act on the first focus of a dialog, Refuse, and never move it; it fails at once on its fact `fails_on`. A test never allows an act or accepts a plan with repeated keys.
