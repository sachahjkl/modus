# The draw table: the 2D interface of the system

The draw table is the one 2D interface of the system: [`docs/PROPERTIES.md`](../../../docs/PROPERTIES.md), "18. One 2D interface: the draw table". A process describes each frame of its window as a table of draw records in its own memory. The drawing module finds the damage by difference and draws the records into the surface. The compositor draws its screen with the same records. When a GPU exists, the GPU draws the same table through WebGPU.

This file records the rationalised form of the record: a closed set of primitives, each record with the fields of its kind only. The guide of today's form is [`docs/INTERFACE.md`](../../../docs/INTERFACE.md), "2.1 Draw record". The border of the drawing module is in [library_modules.md](library_modules.md). The picture memories are in [picture.md](picture.md), and the cells of a grid in [text_grid.md](text_grid.md).

## What changed

Version 10 is a record of 40 bytes with one set of fields for all kinds. A review of it as a general interface found five faults:

- A field changes its meaning with the kind. `second_color` is a color, an ink margin, a stride or a count of columns. `text_start` and `text_count` are also a pixel index and a generation. It is a union with no tag for each field.
- Two kinds were the look of the system, not primitives: `Point_Grid` and `Folded_Gap`.
- Three text kinds overlap: `Text`, `Interface_Text` and `Text_Grid`.
- Primitives are missing: alpha and blending (a color is `0x00RRGGBB`), antialiasing, paths and curves (the vector font has them inside, the table does not), a gradient along a line other than the two axes, a stack of clips (a `Clip` replaces the cut), and places that are not whole pixels.
- The comments of the format tell its history.

## The constraints

These properties of version 10 work, and the new form keeps each one:

- **Fixed-size records in the memory of the caller** (property 14). A table is an array. The difference compares two records at one index. The process allocates nothing.
- **Damage by difference.** Two equal records damage nothing. A record that changes damages its old and its new rectangle. A record must therefore give its rectangle with no other memory, and equal bytes must mean equal pixels.
- **Values, not addresses.** A record names its text, its cells and its pixels by numbers in memory that the call passes. The record means the same in each address space, and a GPU can read it.
- **Refusal of a hostile record with bounds.** A record of a caller can hold any bytes. The execution refuses a record that it cannot draw within a stated cost, and no record makes a fault or a write outside the pixels.
- **The same pixels in each cut.** A frame drawn whole and a frame drawn in its damage rectangles give the same bytes. So the color of a pixel is a function of the records only, never of the rectangle that cuts them.
- **No contract depends on a language** (property 19). The record is bytes at stated offsets, not a type of Odin.

## The chosen form

### The record: a header and the fields of one kind

A draw record is 48 bytes, little-endian: a header of 24 bytes, then 24 bytes that belong to its kind.

| Offset | Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `kind` | u8 | The word of the kind: `Draw_Kind` after the border |
| 1 | `reserved` | 3 x u8 | 0 |
| 4 | `element` | u32 | The low 32 bits of the identity of the element that wrote the record, 0 for none |
| 8 | `box` | 4 x i32 | `left`, `top`, `right`, `bottom`, in units of 1/64 pixel: the record draws no pixel outside it |
| 24 | the fields of the kind | 24 bytes | Each kind below gives its own fields; each byte that the kind does not name is 0 |

- **The box is the one rectangle of the record.** For a rectangle and a gradient it is the shape. For a path, a text, a picture and a grid it holds every pixel that the record draws. The execution cuts each record to its box. A box that is too small therefore cuts only the drawing of its own record, and the damage of a record is its box rounded out to whole pixels: never too small.
- **One border.** The procedure `draw_record.read` of the contract package `draw_record` takes the one copy of a record that its reader made (a volatile read) and gives a `Draw_Primitive`: a union of Odin with one record type for each kind. It refuses a kind word that names no member, a reserved byte that is not 0, a box that is inverted or past the limit, and a field out of its bounds, each with its `Problem`. Past it, the execution and the difference switch on the primitive, whole, with no `#partial` and no default case. `draw_record.record_of` writes the record of a primitive, so the kind of a record always matches its fields. The bytes are the contract; the union is the view of one reader.
- **The record is a contract package.** The writer (the interface package) and the readers (the drawing module, the compositor, the tool that draws the byte layouts of the site, a fuzz target) compile in one form. The described exports name `draw_record.Draw_Record` by its package: the check of the declarations of an import reads the layout of a struct by its name, not through an alias. The box is four fields, not a nested record, because a description holds no record in a record.
- **Unused bytes are 0, and the border checks it.** So two records that draw the same thing have the same bytes, and the comparison of the bytes stays the comparison of the drawing.

### The unit: 1/64 pixel

Each place and each size of a record is a signed number of 1/64 pixel.

- The measure of a text is in 1/64 pixel already: the advances of the interface font, and the right-aligned text of `draw_text_right`. A text needs no conversion, and its origin goes to the nearest third of a pixel as today.
- The rasterizer of the vector font works in 1/256 pixel. A place of a record goes to it with a shift of 2 bits, with no rounding.
- An i32 of 1/64 pixel reaches ±2^25 pixels. Each place of a record stays inside ±2^29 units (`COORDINATE_LIMIT`, 2^23 pixels), so the difference of two places, at most 2^30, fits an i32 and no sum of the execution wraps. A limit of 2^24 pixels was refused: from -2^30 to +2^30 units the width is 2^31, one past the largest i32.
- A shape whose edges are on whole pixels (multiples of 64) has a coverage of 0 or 1 at each pixel: it draws the bytes of version 10.

### The color and the blending rule

A color is a u32 `0xAARRGGBB` with straight alpha (`Draw_Color`). Alpha 255 is opaque. A color of 0 is transparent black, so a record of zeros draws nothing (ZII).

The one blending rule: at each pixel that a record reaches, its cover is the alpha of its color times the coverage of its shape at that pixel. The coverage is 1 inside a shape, a part of 1 on an antialiased edge, and one value for each channel for a glyph with subpixel bands. The color goes over the pixel by source over, in linear light with a gamma of 2, for each channel:

    result = sqrt((d * d * (255 - a) + c * c * a) / 255)

with d the pixel, c the color and a the cover. It is the blend of the text of today (`blend_coverage_row`, [`docs/FONTS.md`](../../../docs/FONTS.md), "3. Subpixel bands"), in integers with the table of square roots. A cover of 255 writes c, with no blend: an opaque record on whole pixels is a fill, as today.

- **One rule for every kind.** The edges of a path, the text, a translucent rectangle and a picture with alpha blend the same way.
- **The colors of a gradient mix in encoded values**, alpha too, as `graphics.mix` mixes them today; then the mixed color blends by the rule. A gradient of opaque colors on whole pixels draws the bytes of version 10.
- **The pixels of a surface stay opaque.** A surface is `0x00RRGGBB`: the window layer paints the background into each damage rectangle before the records draw, so the result of a translucent record is the same in each cut.

### The kinds

`Draw_Kind` is a closed set, with the values of its contract:

| Value | Kind | Fields after the header |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `None` | None: the record draws nothing |
| 1 | `Rectangle` | `color` u32 at 24; `cell_x`, `cell_y` 2 x i32 at 28; `cell_width`, `cell_height`, `step_x`, `step_y` 4 x u16 at 36 |
| 12 | `Gradient` | `start_x`, `start_y`, `end_x`, `end_y` 4 x i32 at 24; `start_color`, `end_color` 2 x u32 at 40 (built; a new value, so that no word of a kind that left names it) |
| 13 | `Path` | `first` u32 at 24, `count` u32 at 28, `fill_color` u32 at 32, `stroke_color` u32 at 36, `stroke_width` u32 at 40 (built; a new value, as for the gradient: the word 3 was the horizontal gradient of version 14, and the word 10 of the line of version 15 names no kind) |
| 4 | `Text` | `first` u32 at 24, `count` u32 at 28, `color` u32 at 32, `origin_x` i32 at 36, `font` u8 at 40, `weight` u8 at 41, `size` u16 at 42 |
| 8 | `Picture` | `memory` u8 at 24, `format` u8 at 25, `first` u32 at 28, `stride` u32 at 32, `width`, `height` 2 x u32 at 36, `generation` u32 at 44 |
| 14 | `Clip_Push` | None: the box is the clip (built; a new value, as for the gradient: the word 5 was the `Clip` of version 16, which replaced the cut) |
| 15 | `Clip_Pop` | None, and a box of zeros |
| 9 | `Text_Grid` | `first` u32 at 24, `count` u32 at 28, `columns` u32 at 32, `size` u16 at 36; kept until the measure below decides |

Two more kinds are designed, not built: `Element_Push` (16) and `Element_Pop` (17), which draw nothing and give the records between them an element role, its states and an accessible name. A record under an open element push carries the element word of the innermost push, or 0, and the border of the order checks it, with the clips, in one order. The design is [../features/accessibility.md](../features/accessibility.md), "1. The semantic tree".

**Rectangle.** The box in one color. With `step_x` and `step_y` above 0, the rectangle repeats: a cell of `cell_width` by `cell_height` at (`cell_x` + n × `step_x`, `cell_y` + m × `step_y`) of the surface for each integers n and m, cut to the box. A step of 0 on both axes is one rectangle, the whole box, and then each field of the pattern is 0. The border refuses a cell or a step below 1 pixel, a step below the cell, one step of 0 with the other above 0, and a place past `COORDINATE_LIMIT`. So a rectangle reaches each pixel at most one time, and its pixel writes are at most the pixels of its box. A repeated rectangle is a pattern: points, stripes, the lines of a grid.

The bound in calls of a fill: cells that touch are one fill. A cell as wide as its step makes each row of cells one span across the area, a cell as high as its step makes each column one span down it, and both make the area one fill, as a solid rectangle. The most calls come from a cell of 1 pixel each 2 pixels on each axis: one call for each cell, a quarter of the pixels of the area, about 518 000 calls of one pixel for an area of 1920 by 1080. With a cell as wide as its step and a step of 2 down, it is one call for each second line: 540 calls. The border does not refuse a dense pattern, nor a cell of 1 each 2: each is a look, and the format bounds a cost, never a look ("The limits of the format: cost, never look"). The sizes are u16 in 1/64 pixel, so a step is at most 1 023 pixels, and the record keeps 24 bytes of fields.

The pattern is placed on the surface by one of its cells, not on the box: two boxes of one surface, two damage rectangles and two areas show one pattern, and an element that draws the pattern in a part of a surface needs no clip and no second record. A pattern placed at the top left corner of its box was refused: the box of the grid of points would then start before its area, at the point of the grid before it, and the element would need a clip around it, three records in place of one, and a clip that replaces the cut of its caller until the clip stack exists.

**Gradient.** The box in a color that goes from `start_color` at the start point to `end_color` at the end point. The color of a pixel comes from the projection of its center on the line of the two points, cut to the two ends, in steps of 1/255. A start point equal to the end point is refused, and so are two points more than `GRADIENT_SPAN_LIMIT` apart on an axis, so that each product of the level fits 64 bits. A gradient down on whole pixels gives the bytes of the vertical gradient of version 10; a gradient across gives each channel of the horizontal gradient within 2, because that kind stepped each channel in fixed point with one rounding, where the steps of 1/255 round the level down and then the mix down: the two roundings add up. Both keep their fast form: one mix for each line, or one row of colors that each line copies when it is opaque.

**Path.** A list of points in the byte table: `count` bytes from the byte `first`, a multiple of 4, `PATH_POINT_SIZE` (12) bytes for each point. A point (`Path_Point_Record`) is a word of `Path_Command`, then `x` and `y`, i32 in 1/64 pixel of the surface, the unit of each place of a record. The commands are a closed set with the values of the contract, and 0 names none, so a range of zeros is no path:

| Value | Command | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Move` | Starts a subpath at the point |
| 2 | `Line` | A line segment from the point before to the point; also the end of a curve |
| 3 | `Curve_Control` | The control point of a quadratic curve; the next point, a `Line`, is its end |
| 4 | `Close` | Closes the subpath with a segment back to its `Move`; its `x` and `y` are 0 |

- **The fill** is in `fill_color` by the nonzero rule, and closes each subpath. The coverage of a pixel is the sum of the signed areas of the segments, then its absolute value cut to one pixel. A pixel with no edge in it is covered whole exactly when its winding number is not 0: the nonzero rule. A pixel with an edge takes the part of it inside, and where two edges of opposite winding cross inside one pixel, their areas subtract before the cut, an error inside that one pixel. The nonzero rule is chosen because the fonts and the pointer theme are outlines of that rule, and because the sum of areas gives it with no state: the even-odd rule needs the parity of the winding of each pixel, which the sum does not hold.
- **The stroke** is `stroke_width` wide, in `stroke_color`, along each segment of each subpath, curves cut first: butt ends, and a bevel join at each point between two segments. A subpath that ends with `Close` is closed for the stroke: the segment back to its `Move`, and a join at its two ends. The stroke is a filled outline: one band of four corners for each segment, all of one direction of area, and one triangle for each join, of the same direction, so the sum of their areas is at least one pixel wherever two overlap, and the cut to one pixel blends each pixel once. Butt ends and bevel joins are the smallest set that draws any polyline with no gap: a miter needs a limit and a second form, a round join and a round end need curves of their own; each comes when a process needs it. `draw_line` gets the square ends of the line of before by moving its two points half the width out.
- **The colors and the width are canonical.** A `fill_color` or a `stroke_color` of alpha 0 is 0. No stroke has one form: `stroke_color` 0 and `stroke_width` 0. So the border refuses a width of 0 with a color, a color 0 with a width, and a width past `STROKE_WIDTH_LIMIT`.
- **A curve** is cut into the fewest segments, 1 to `CURVE_SEGMENT_LIMIT` (16), whose distance from the curve is at most `CURVE_TOLERANCE_UNITS` (8 units, 1/8 pixel, the tolerance of the glyphs). With n segments of equal steps of the parameter, the distance is at most |start − 2 control + end| / (4 n²): a curve whose bend, the sum of the two axes of that vector, is at most 8 192 units (128 pixels) is within the tolerance; past it, the 16 segments are within its bend / 1 024 units (`curve_segment_count`). Each point of the cut comes from the three points of the curve, rounded to the unit, so no error adds up along the curve (`curve_point`). The cut is in the contract: a back end on a GPU cuts a curve the same way.
- **The fill draws first, then the stroke over it**, each with the one blending rule: the alpha of its color times the coverage of each pixel, `(alpha × coverage + 4096) >> 13` with a pixel covered whole at 8 192. A pixel covered whole is the color times its alpha, and a pixel covered x by y units has the alpha of a `Rectangle` that covers that part: an axis-aligned square path draws the pixels of the `Rectangle` of its box.
- **Two borders.** `draw_record.read` checks the record: a first byte that is not a multiple of 4, a count that is not a whole count of points, fewer than 2 or more than `PATH_POINT_LIMIT` (1 024) points, a color of alpha 0 that is not 0, and the stroke above. The points are in the byte table, not in the record, so they have their own border, `draw_record.path_points_read`, which the reader calls on its one copy of them: a word that names no command (`Unknown_Command`), a point past `COORDINATE_LIMIT` (`Point_Past_Coordinate_Limit`), a `Close` off 0 (`Close_Point_Not_Zero`), a first point that is not a `Move` (`Not_Started_By_Move`), a `Move` with no segment after it (`Empty_Subpath`), a `Curve_Control` with no `Line` after it (`Curve_Without_End`), a `Close` that does not follow a `Line` or that a point other than a `Move` follows (`Close_Out_Of_Place`). The reader also refuses a range past the byte table and a range at an address that is not a multiple of 4. A path refused by either draws nothing.
- **The canonical form** that the borders keep: each subpath starts with its `Move` and has a segment, each curve has its end, a `Close` ends its subpath, and the colors and the width have one form each. Two paths with other points can still draw the same pixels (a point that a segment of no length repeats, a `Close` against a last `Line` to the `Move`, two subpaths in another order): the damage is too large there, never too small, as for the other kinds.
- **The box** is on whole pixels, as for each kind but a rectangle and a gradient, and cuts the path. The points lie inside a pixel; a box inside a pixel would need the coverage of the box times the coverage of the shape, for no need. The writer makes the box the box of the points, the control points too, since a curve stays inside the box of its three points, grown by half the stroke and rounded out.
- **The cost is bounded.** This is the one statement of the bound; the other places name it. At most 1 024 points. A curve gives at most 16 segments for its 2 points, a `Line` and a `Close` 1 segment each, a `Move` none: at most 8 segments for each point, 8 192 in all. The stroke of a segment is 4 edges for its band and 3 for its join with the segment before; the segment of a `Close` is 10, its band and a join at each of its two ends. So a curve gives the most edges for each point, 16 × 7 / 2 = 56, and a stroke has at most 56 × 1 024 = 57 344 edges (`PATH_EDGE_LIMIT`); the fill has at most 8 192 edges and one more for each subpath, fewer. The work of a draw is the bands of its cut times those edges, and each edge adds to the rows of a band and to the columns that it crosses there. A stroke is at most 64 pixels wide (`STROKE_WIDTH_LIMIT`), so it grows the box by at most 32 pixels on each side.

**Text.** One text form for every font. The UTF-8 bytes of the run are in the byte table: `count` bytes from `first`, at most `INTERFACE_TEXT_LIMIT` (256) in the interface font. `font` is a `Font_Kind`: `Cells_8x16` 0, `Interface` 1. `origin_x` is the left of the advance box, in 1/64 pixel, and the top of the box is the top of the line: the font of the cells puts the origin on a whole pixel, the interface font on a third of a pixel, the three positions of a glyph. `weight` is a `Font_Weight`, at each size. `size` is the text size, the height of the line in 1/64 pixel, from `TEXT_SIZE_MINIMUM` (256: 4 pixels) to `TEXT_SIZE_LIMIT` (16 384: 256 pixels); the border refuses another size, 0 too, and never clamps it. The box holds the ink, with the ink margin of the font at the size: the field `second_color` of version 10 that held the margin is gone, because the box says it. The origin is at most the larger of 16 pixels and one line inside the left of the box. The design of the size is in [../features/text_rendering.md](../features/text_rendering.md), "The draw record".

`Text` and `Interface_Text` of version 10 were one concept with two places for the origin. A text in the font of the cells is a text run whose font is the font of the cells.

**Picture.** The pixels of a picture of the caller, by values in its picture memory, as in version 10 ([picture.md](picture.md)): `memory` is the number of the picture memory, `first` its first pixel, `stride` the pixels from one line to the next, `generation` a new number when its pixels change. `format` is a `Picture_Format`: `Opaque` 0, which reads `0x00RRGGBB` and copies it, and `Straight_Alpha` 1, which reads `0xAARRGGBB` and blends by the rule. The box is the place of the picture and its size: whole pixels, and exactly `width` by `height` pixels; another box is refused. A picture is drawn at its size: no scale and no repetition.

**Clip_Push and Clip_Pop.** A `Clip_Push` makes the cut of the records after it the intersection of its box with the cut before it, until its `Clip_Pop`. A `Clip_Pop` gives back the cut before the last push that is open. So an element that clips inside a clipped area keeps the outer clip, and a nested clip larger than its parent is cut to its parent. A list inside a scrolled card writes no intersection by hand.

- **Two kinds, not a field.** A clip in each record (a box or an index of a clip in the header) costs bytes in every record, and the difference would compare the clip of each record. A push and a pop are one record each for a group of records. Neither has a field: the box of a push is the clip, and a pop has a box of zeros, so each has one form of bytes.
- **Whole pixels.** The box of a push is on whole pixels, as the box of each kind but a rectangle and a gradient. A clip with an edge inside a pixel needs a coverage for each pixel, multiplied into each record under it: a mask, a cost on each pixel of each kind. A GPU cuts by a scissor rectangle, which is whole pixels. A form with an edge inside a pixel is a path.
- **The depth bound is a cost.** At most `CLIP_DEPTH_LIMIT` (16) pushes are open at a time. A reader keeps one rectangle for each depth, with no allocator: 17 rectangles of 32 bytes on its stack, 544 bytes whatever the table. A push is one intersection and a pop one step down, at each depth, so the bound bounds the memory, not the work. The elements of the system nest 4 deep at most (a window, a card, a list, a row). The bound is never a rule of the look ("The limits of the format: cost, never look").
- **Two borders.** The border of a record (`read`) checks a push as each record on whole pixels: a box that is inverted, past `COORDINATE_LIMIT`, or off the whole pixels. It refuses a pop whose box or fields are not 0 (`Reserved_Not_Zero`: a pop names no byte of its box). The order of the clips is a fact of the table, not of one record: the border of the order, `clip_order_check`, walks the kind words of a table and refuses a pop with no push open (`Pop_Without_Push`), a push past the bound (`Push_Past_Depth_Limit`), and a table that ends with a push open (`Push_Without_Pop`), with the index of the record: the outermost push open, for the end. The drawing module runs it before the first pixel: a refused table draws nothing, and the export gives -1 and the error record `Bad_Value` of unit `Records`, with the word of the problem as `value` and the number from 1 of the record as `found`.
- **The order is the order of the kind words.** A record whose kind word is `Clip_Push` is a push, also when the border of the record refuses it: a refused push is an empty clip, so the records under it draw nothing until its pop. A refused pop still ends its push. So the depth at each record depends on the kind words only, and the border of the order, the execution and the difference agree on it. A refused clip never widens a cut.
- **The execution walks the order again.** A table can change during the call, after its border of the order passed (a count from another party is copied, then checked, and never read again). The execution keeps its own order with each record that it reads, and draws less, never outside a clip that the table pushed: a pop with no push draws nothing and keeps the cut; a push past the bound is counted and not held, and the records under it draw nothing until its pop; a push open at the end changes no pixel. The package compiled into a process (`execute_in_rectangle`) gives no status: these rules are all it applies.
- **The writer** (`draw_clip_push`, `draw_clip_pop`) writes no table that the border of the order refuses. Each record that a table takes keeps room for the pops of the pushes open, so a pop always fits. A push past the bound is not written and sets `refused`, a push that does not fit sets `full`, and the pop of a push that is not written is not written. The records under a push that is not written are not written either, and set `refused`: they draw nothing, as the execution draws nothing under a push past the bound, and none draws outside the clip that its writer asked for. A pop with no push open is not written and sets `refused`. A part of a table that a caller draws alone (`draw_part_through_module`) holds whole pairs of a push and its pop, since the module checks the order of the part it is given. `frame_end` writes the pops of the pushes that a frame left open, and sets `refused`. A push with a negative width or height is an empty clip.
- **The lists push the clip of their area.** `list_with_choice` and `list_of_rows` write a push of their area before their rows and its pop after their scroll bar: a list whose area is shorter than one line drew that line past its area, and now draws none of it outside. A list inside a clipped card keeps the clip of the card. Two records more for each list.
- **The input is not cut.** A clip cuts what the records draw, not where the pointer acts: each element takes the pointer in its own area, as before.

**Text_Grid.** The grid of cells of the terminal, as in version 10 ([text_grid.md](text_grid.md)): the cells in the byte table, 16 bytes each, with their colors as draw colors. `size` is its text size, with the bounds of a text: a cell is half the size wide, a half up, and two widths high. It stays its own kind only if a measure shows the need (below).

### The byte table

The field `text` of `Draw_Table` is the byte table. It holds the bytes that the records name: the UTF-8 of the texts, the cells of the grids and the points of the paths. Cells and points start at a byte that is a multiple of 4, in a table at an address that is a multiple of 4. The difference compares two records with their `first` fields masked, then compares the bytes that each names, as it compares the bytes of a text. Two paths with the same points in another place of the table are equal.

The field keeps its name `text` and its exports their parameter `now_text`. A rename to `bytes` was refused in the step of the paths: it changes each window, the drawing module, its exports and the description of each importer, and buys no pixel; the guides name the concept the byte table.

### The look of the system: elements, not kinds

`Point_Grid` and `Folded_Gap` leave the format. Each becomes an element of the interface package, drawn with the primitives. The tokens of their colors stay in the package, where the appearance in force chooses them ([../features/appearance.md](../features/appearance.md)).

- **The grid of points** (`background` and `draw_point_grid`): one repeated `Rectangle` of `colors.point`, whose box is the area, with the pattern `POINT_GRID_PATTERN`: a cell of `POINT_SIZE` at `POINT_STEP / 2` of the surface, one each `POINT_STEP`. One record, as the kind `Point_Grid` was. The points stay at the same places of the surface, so two areas of one surface show one grid.
- **The folded gap** (`draw_folded_gap`): a `Rectangle` of `colors.track`, then one `Path` of two subpaths, the two lines stroked 1 pixel wide in `colors.dim`, both with the box of the gap, so the lines are cut to the gap as the kind `Folded_Gap` cut them. Two records in place of one.
- **The line** (`draw_line`): a `Path` of two points, stroked. Its points are the centers of the squares that the line of Bresenham drew at its two ends, moved half the thickness out along the line, so a line across or down covers the whole pixels that it covered before, and a line at an angle is an antialiased stroke. Its box holds the four corners of its band, which the execution computes from the same two points.

### The limits of the format: cost, never look

The format puts no limit of look on what a process draws. Each bound of a kind (`PATH_POINT_LIMIT`, `STROKE_WIDTH_LIMIT`, `INTERFACE_TEXT_LIMIT`, `TEXT_SIZE_LIMIT`, `CLIP_DEPTH_LIMIT`, `COORDINATE_LIMIT`) is there to bound the cost of a hostile record, and for no other reason. The look of the desktop of the system is sober by choice: rectangles, sharp shadows, transparency only where it informs, short animations that inform ([`docs/DESIGN.md`](../../../docs/DESIGN.md), "2. Forms"). That look is a rule of the elements of the interface package and of the compositor. It is never a reason to refuse a primitive.

- A process can draw any form: round, at an angle, curved. A round form is a path.
- A shadow is a rectangle or a path with alpha. A sharp shadow needs no new kind.
- An effect that the set lacks today (a blur, a blend mode, an even-odd fill, a cubic curve, a round join, a dash, a gradient of more than two colors) is added when a process needs it, as a kind or a field under the version rule. A blur waits for the GPU, where it costs little; on the processor it is the largest cost of a frame.
- The set stays closed: each kind that is added has its fields, its border and its bound of cost, as each kind of today.

### The text grid: the measure that decides

A grid as text runs needs one `Text` record for each run of one color in each row, and a `Rectangle` for each run of reverse or filled cells. A plain terminal is one run for each row: about 32 records. The worst case is one run for each cell: 3 328 runs and up to 3 328 rectangles, past the 512 records of a usual window memory.

The measure: the terminal at 104 by 32 cells, in the frame of a plain text, of colored code, and of one color for each cell, in both forms, through the drawing module. It gives the records, the bytes of the two tables, and the time of the build, of the difference and of the draw. The rule: the grid stays its own kind if the form in runs costs more than 1 ms more for one frame in the worst case, or needs more than 4 096 records. Else the kind goes, and the terminal draws runs. Until the measure, the grid stays: it works today.

### The version rule

`DRAW_RECORD_VERSION` is the version of the form of the record. Each change of a kind, of a field, of a bound that changes what a record draws, or of the blending rule is a new version. The format changes in place: the package, the drawing module and each writer move in the same step, and no reader reads two versions. The names of the exports carry the version (`damage_of_draw_table_<version>`, `execute_draw_table_in_rectangles_<version>`): a module of another version has no export with the name that an importer binds, so the load fails before the check of the descriptions ([`docs/INTERFACE.md`](../../../docs/INTERFACE.md), "7. Where it lives: the elements in each process, the drawing in a module"). A constant check in the package and in the module stops a build whose names do not follow the version.

### The execution

**The drawing module** keeps its two exports and the calls of a frame: one for the difference, one for the draw of all its damage rectangles.

- The rasterizer of a path works in bands of the cut, `PATH_BAND_ROWS` (8) rows by `PATH_BAND_COLUMNS` (256) columns: an accumulation of one i32 cell for each pixel of the band and one more on each row. Its memory is on the stack of the caller, a fixed size whatever the cut: 12 KiB for the one copy of the points, 8 KiB for the band, 1 KiB for one row of colors, 21 KiB in all, under the 64 KiB of the smallest stack of a thread that draws. The procedure that draws a path is never inlined, so its scratch is in its own frame, and the draw of each other kind keeps a small one. A parameter `work: Bytes(Change)` of the caller was refused: it changes the export and each caller for memory that a band of fixed size holds on the stack.
- Before the first rectangle, the module runs the border of the order of the clips on the table. For each damage rectangle, it paints the background when the shape asks for it, then reads each record through the border from the first record of the table, keeps the clip stack, and draws each record whose box meets the cut, cut to it. The cut at a depth is the rectangle cut by the box of each push open: a function of the records only, so the pixels are the same in each cut.
- A path is drawn again in each rectangle that its box meets. The coverage of each pixel comes from every segment of the path, also the segments outside the cut: a piece left of a band adds its height to the first cell of its row, a piece right of it adds nothing there, and a segment above or below adds nothing. The cut of a piece at the edge of a band is the cut of the whole piece at that column, a function of the segment and of the line of the cut only, in the coordinates of the surface. So each band gives each of its pixels the sum that one band over the whole box gives, and the bytes are the same in each cut. A clamp of the places to the band, as the glyphs clamp to their box, was refused: it bends a segment that crosses the edge of the band, so the pixels would depend on the cut.
- The sums are in i32: an edge gives one piece to each row, a piece adds at most one pixel (8 192) to the sums of its row, and a pass has at most `PATH_EDGE_LIMIT` (57 344) edges (the bound of the cost of a path, under the kinds above), so a sum stays under 57 344 × 8 192, below 2^29; a constant check in `path.odin` stops a build past 2^31. The places are in i64: a point and its stroke reach 2^30 units from the cut, and a product of two differences reaches 2^61. The rasterizer of the glyphs works in i32 of 1/256 pixel inside the box of one glyph; a path needs the range of the record, so the path has its own rasterizer of the same method in the interface package (`path.odin`).
- The difference compares two tables at one index. Two pushes, or two pops, keep the order of the clips. Two pushes whose boxes differ damage the two boxes, as two other records do: a record under them draws inside them only, so each pixel that the change moves is in one of the two boxes. A push or a pop at one index where the other table has another kind changes the depth of each record after it: each record from there to the end of the two tables is damaged, except the clips, which draw nothing. So two tables whose kind words agree at each index have the same depth at each index, also with refused records, a push past the bound or a pop with no push. The damage is never too small; it is too large where a push moves and the records under it do not.

**The compositor** draws the parts of its screen with the same export, one call for each part ([picture.md](picture.md), "The compositor: one call for each part"). The surface of a window is an `Opaque` picture. With alpha, a menu, a shadow or the palette can lie translucent over the windows, by the one rule.

**Through a GPU, later.** When a GPU exists, a back end of the drawing module draws the same table through the WebGPU subset of the system, whose shaders the build compiles from WGSL to SPIR-V ([../features/gpu_access.md](../features/gpu_access.md)):

- each record is one instance over its box; a rectangle and a gradient are a quad with the coverage of its edges in the shader;
- a path gets its coverage in a pass of its own, as a glyph gets it; a text reads the glyph caches in a texture;
- the clip stack is a scissor rectangle, which is why a clip is on whole pixels;
- the rule in linear light with a gamma of 2 is not the sRGB curve of the hardware: the pass draws into a target of 16-bit floats that holds the squares of the values, blends there with the source over of the hardware, and a last pass takes the square roots.

The GPU gives the same picture within an error that a test states, not the same bytes. The damage stays the damage of the records: the GPU draws in the damage rectangles only. The table, the difference and the elements do not change.

## The refused forms

- **A software adapter of WebGPU as the 2D interface.** It needs a compiler of WGSL for the processor, larger than the whole 2D drawing, and it draws a rectangle through a shader. The draw table is drawn through WebGPU when a GPU exists, not the other way round. The processor back end of the WebGPU subset, for 3D, is not this form: its shaders are a fixed set in Odin, and the draw table never goes through it ([../features/desktop_to_browser.md](../features/desktop_to_browser.md), "F. 3D").
- **A union of the language as the contract.** The layout and the tag of a `union` of Odin belong to the compiler, and property 19 forbids a contract that depends on one language. The record is bytes with an explicit kind; the union is the view past the border.
- **Records of variable size**, as a stream of commands. The difference compares two tables at one index, and a table of fixed records has that index with no parse. A length in each record is one more field that a hostile caller sets.
- **One record with the fields of every kind**, the form of version 10. It is the union with no tag that the review found.
- **Floating-point places.** Two equal drawings must have equal bytes; a float has two zeros and many NaN. The rasterizer works in integers, so its pixels are the same at each build. The base types of a contract have no float (property 19, "Open").
- **1/16 pixel.** It loses two bits of the measure of a text, which is in 1/64, so a right-aligned text could move by a third of a pixel. **1/256 pixel**, the unit of the rasterizer, gives no visible gain and a range four times smaller.
- **Premultiplied alpha in a record.** A transparent color then has many forms, and a token of the appearance names a straight color. The execution multiplies.
- **The blend in encoded values for the colors**, as the pointer pictures blend. It darkens the edges of a shape and of a text, which is why the text blends in linear light today. One rule serves every kind.
- **Blend modes past source over, in this version.** No process needs one yet. One comes as a field under the version rule when a process needs it ("The limits of the format: cost, never look").
- **The even-odd rule, cubic curves, miter joins, round joins and ends, square ends, dashes, in this version.** The fonts and the pointer theme are quadratic outlines with the nonzero rule, and a writer cuts a cubic curve into quadratic curves within a tolerance. Each comes when a process needs it. The look of the desktop is never the reason: it binds the elements, not the format.
- **The points of a path in the fields of its record.** 24 bytes hold two points: a path would be many records, each with its box, and the difference would compare pieces of one shape at many indexes. The byte table holds a list of any length up to its bound, as it holds a text.
- **A subpath closed by a last point equal to its first.** A stroke would then join two ends that a caller placed on one point by chance, and the same points would have two meanings. An explicit `Close` says it, and its place of zeros keeps one form.
- **A stroke width of 0 with a color as no stroke.** Two records that draw nothing would differ by a color; the border refuses it, so no stroke has one form.
- **A path whose curve has a far control point refused.** A far control point is a look, and its cost is bounded by the 16 segments of the cut; the error past 128 pixels of bend is stated instead.
- **The `Line` kind kept beside the stroke.** Two forms of one line, with two sets of pixels: the line of Bresenham drew squares that overlap, so a translucent line blended twice, and a line at an angle had no antialiased edge. A line is a path of one segment.
- **A coverage by the distance to the outline, or by samples in each pixel.** The distance gives no exact area at a corner, and the samples are an estimate whose cost grows with their count; the sum of exact areas is the method of the glyphs, in integers, the same at each build.
- **A limit of the format that is a rule of the look.** It would make the one 2D interface of the system unfit for a module of a third party, whose look is its own.
- **A clip by a path, or a clip with edges inside a pixel.** It needs a mask for each pixel, and a GPU cuts by a scissor rectangle. A form with a curved edge is a path.
- **A clip that replaces the cut**, the `Clip` of version 16, with a box of no width as the end of each cut. An element that clipped inside a clipped area removed the outer clip, and each caller that nested two clips wrote their intersection by hand and wrote the outer clip again after.
- **A clip in each record**, as a box or as an index of a table of clips. It adds bytes to each record for the few records that clip, and an index is one more value that a hostile caller sets.
- **A pop that names its push**, by an index. The order of the kind words already gives each pop its push; an index is one more value to check, with no gain.
- **A refused push that is not a push.** Its pop would end the clip of its parent, and the records after it would draw outside the parent. A refused push is an empty clip.
- **A push past the bound that replaces the top of the stack, or that is ignored.** The records under it would draw outside the clip that their writer asked for. They draw nothing.
- **The order checked only by the execution, record by record.** An honest caller with a table that ends with a push open would never be told. The border of the order gives it an error record before any pixel.
- **The order checked only before the draw.** The table can change during the call; the execution keeps the order again with the records that it reads.
- **A stack in memory of the caller, or with no bound.** The reader would need memory of a size that a hostile table sets.
- **A gradient with more than two colors, or a gradient that fills a path, in this version.** No process needs one yet. Each is a list of variable length or a second kind of paint for each shape; a need comes first.
- **The point grid as a repeated picture.** The tile of pixels must be in the one picture memory of the call, which the process owns: the interface package has no place for it. A repeated rectangle needs no pixels.
- **`Point_Grid` and `Folded_Gap` kept as kinds.** The look of the system is in the elements of the package; the format holds primitives only.
- **The old and the new form side by side in the drawing module.** The rule of the project is no backward compatibility: change a format in place and remove the old path. Each step below changes the format in place, with its version, and each writer moves in the same step.

## The subtle points

- **A box that lies.** A box that is smaller than the drawing cuts the drawing; a box that is larger damages more. Neither harms another record or another process.
- **A transparent record still damages its box.** A change of a color from opaque to transparent changes pixels.
- **A record that the border refuses** draws nothing; a refused push is an empty clip, and a refused pop still ends its push. It is equal to no record whose bytes differ, so a change to or from it damages both boxes; the same refused bytes in two frames draw nothing in both and damage nothing. Its fields are not trusted, so its box is the box of its bytes, cut to `COORDINATE_LIMIT`.
- **The cost of a hostile record is bounded.** A rectangle reaches each pixel of its box at most once. A path has at most 1 024 points, and its work is bounded by the bands of its cut times the edges of its outline (the bound of the cost of a path, under the kinds above). A stroke is at most 64 pixels wide.
- **The points of a path are loaded once.** A table can be in memory that another process writes during a call. The border of the points loads each point once into the copy of the reader, and the fill and the stroke read the copy only. A text is at most 256 bytes. The clip stack is at most 16 deep.
- **The record of zeros is valid.** It is a `None` with an empty box. A zero color is transparent, a zero step is no repetition, a zero stroke color and width are no stroke, a zero font is the font of the cells, a zero format is an opaque picture. A point of zeros names no command, so a range of zeros is no path.
- **The pointer is not a draw record.** The compositor draws the pointer after the last call of a frame, with the blend of its premultiplied pictures in encoded values ([`docs/POINTERS.md`](../../../docs/POINTERS.md)). That second blend stays outside the draw table until the pointer becomes a record.
- **Two spaces, each for one reason.** The colors of a gradient mix in encoded values so that the vertical gradients of before keep their bytes; the cover blends in linear light so that edges and text keep their weight.
- **The kind of a record is read one time.** The border takes one copy of the 48 bytes, and every check and every draw reads that copy: a caller that changes its table during a call changes nothing that was checked.

A writer gives no record to a text in that font whose left is past `COORDINATE_LIMIT`, as it already did for the interface font: version 12 cut that box to the limit and drew the text there, where no surface reaches.
- **The interface font** keeps its origin on a third of a pixel, the ink margin right of the left edge, and its 256 bytes (`INTERFACE_TEXT_LIMIT`).
- **The border** refuses a font word that names no member, and the word 11 of the kind `Interface_Text`, which left the format, as unknown.
- **The pixels** of each text are those of its kind in version 12: a host test draws each text in both fonts, at each scale, weight and third of a pixel, whole and in four damage rectangles, and compares every pixel with the drawing of the old kind.
- **The cost**: the execution switches on the font of each text, and the font of the cells reads its origin in place of the left of its box. Both are one branch and one shift for each text record, to measure with `clock` and `interface_elements`.

Before it, version 12 of the record. The look of the system left the format: the grid of points is one `Rectangle` with a pattern placed on the surface, and the folded gap is a `Rectangle` and two `Line` records with the box of the gap, both drawn by elements of the interface package with the pixels of the kinds that they replaced. The words 6 and 7 of those kinds name no kind, and the border refuses them as unknown. The pixels can differ in three cases:

- a folded gap narrower than 6 pixels, whose line the execution draws from the other end (the process `inspect` draws gaps of 16 pixels);
- a grid of points in an area left of or above the pixel -10 of a surface, where the pattern has cells that the kind did not draw;
- a folded gap with an end of a line past `LINE_COORDINATE_LIMIT` (2^15 pixels): its track is drawn, and the line is left out, where the kind drew its lines up to `COORDINATE_LIMIT` (2^23 pixels). No surface of the system reaches 2^15 pixels.

A pattern draws the cells that touch as one fill, so a dense pattern costs one call, and the worst is a cell of 1 pixel each 2 pixels.

Before it, version 11 of the record, the tagged form: the header with the box in 1/64 pixel and the fields of each kind, in the contract package `src/library/contract/draw_record`, with its border, the writer of each kind, its byte layouts on the site and its fuzz target. Two fields of version 10 became places: the ink margin of a text in the interface font is the distance from the left edge of its box to its origin, which is a place in 1/64 pixel on a third of a pixel; a line holds its two ends, and its box holds each square of its path. A record that the border refuses damages its box when its bytes change: in version 10 a refused line damaged nothing. The border refuses a color whose high byte is not 0, so a record that it reads is canonical, and the difference compares the 48 bytes of two records first and reads through the border only the records that differ. `COORDINATE_LIMIT` is 2^23 pixels, so the width of the widest box in units fits an i32.
